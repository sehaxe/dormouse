# Ripple-65B Preview: what a 2026 frontier open-weight release changes for us

**Source:** https://www.ricursive.com/ripple, published 2026-11-06. [T] for the
company's own numbers, [C] for arithmetic I did on them, [S] for inference.

Why it matters: it is the closest 2026 release to our own thesis — a **dense**
model, thin-and-deep, 1M context, trained for **efficiency per dollar** rather
than raw scale, and it came out **US open-weight SOTA on agentic coding** by
beating models 8-15× its size. It is also the first frontier release that
publishes its *recipe-laddering* result, which is exactly the lever we can
afford.

## The numbers

| | value | source |
|---|---:|---|
| parameters | 65B, dense, 80 layers, d_model 5120 | [T] |
| tokens | 12T | [T] |
| context | 1M | [T] |
| pretraining MFU | 40% | [T] |
| total R&D cost | $32M (from scratch, incl. ladders, data, post-training) | [T] |
| TerminalBench 2.1 | 80.52 (prior US open-weight SOTA 80.1 at 501B) | [T] |
| RL rollouts | 1.4M, ~70× fewer than the prior SOTA | [T] |
| **compute** | **6·65e9·12e12 = 4.68e24 FLOPs** | [C] |
| **GPU-hours at 40% MFU** (H100 bf16 dense, 989 TFLOP/s) | **3.3M** ≈ $6.6M at $2/h | [C] |
| tokens per parameter | **185** (Chinchilla is 20) | [C] |

Our card for comparison: 45.7 TFLOP/s peak × 13.9% measured MFU = 6.35 TFLOP/s.
Their run on our card would take **205 000 GPU-hours = 23 years**. Not
transferable. What *is* transferable is the method and the ratio.

## What it confirms about our own choices

1. **Dense over MoE at this scale.** They chose dense explicitly "rather than
   use Mixture-of-Experts like other labs", for infrastructure simplicity, and
   got 40% MFU with simple sharding and no custom kernels. Our 13.5M dense
   control beating the 102.7M KDA flagship by 0.126-0.37 BPB is the same
   finding at 10× smaller scale. Component #10 of `the-design.md` (fine-grained
   MoE) is now a **bet against the current open-weight SOTA**, not a bet with it.
2. **Thin and deep.** 5120/80 = aspect ratio 64. Ours is 832/12 = 69 — the same
   shape, 7× shallower. Their depth choice is not exotic; ours is consistent.
3. **1M context is table stakes in 2026.** A 65B dense model ships it. Our 34 MB
   state for 1M remains a genuine advantage (theirs stores KV), but "1M context"
   alone is no longer a differentiator — the memory cost is.
4. **Recipe beats architecture, and by a lot.** Their ladder v1.0→v1.6
   (StepFun batch/LR scaling, pre+post norm, sigmoid gated attention, global
   NoPE, LR cooldown to zero, value residuals, code upsampling) compounded to
   **16× compute efficiency at 4B on dolma_stack CE** [T]. Our own ledger says
   the same: the Muon fix was worth −0.267 BPB, more than every architectural
   delta we have measured combined. **The next 16× is in the recipe, not in a
   new attention variant.**

## What it changes in `2026-11-08-the-design.md`

| component | before | after this release |
|---|---|---|
| #6 Muon shape-fair + bf16 state | proven | unchanged |
| #10 fine-grained MoE | bet | **downgraded**: the 2026 open-weight SOTA is dense; MoE is now a contrarian bet, not a default |
| mHC (our experimental arm) | unproven | **externally rejected at 4B** ("good at smaller scale, did not improve at 4B") — our mHC results stay small-scale-only claims |

## The six cheap knobs, and whether we have them

Verified by grep against `src/dormouse/config.py` and `model/transformer.py`:

| knob | have it? | cost to add | published effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| value residuals | **no** | ~10 lines in `TransformerBlock._block` (carry the first layer's `v` into later layers' attention) | v1.6, part of the 16× |
| sigmoid gated attention | **no** (only inside `dspark`/`engram`) | ~5 lines: `y = y * sigmoid(W_g x)` | v1.3, part of the 16× |
| FFN activation clipping | **no** | 1 line: `h = h.clamp(max=c)` before `fc2` | GPT-OSS recipe |
| global NoPE | **no** (RoPE everywhere, 67 references) | medium: per-layer rope on/off | v1.5, part of the 16× |
| LR cooldown to zero | **partially**: `wsd_lr(final_frac=0.1)` decays to 10%, and `final_frac=0.0` is asserted away | 2 lines (allow 0.0) + a config default | v1.5, part of the 16× |
| code upsampling in the mix | **yes** (`configs/mix_v7*.toml`) | — | final version |
| pre+post norm | **no** (plain pre-norm) | medium | neutral below 4B — skip |

The first five are cheap, independent, and each has an A/B at 95M that costs
1-2 GPU-hours. That is the correct next architectural spend — not another
attention mechanism.

## The data-ratio finding, which is the one that hurts

Ripple trained **185 tokens per parameter**, 9.2× beyond Chinchilla, because at
their scale serving cost dominates and a denser (smaller) model is cheaper to
run. At *our* scale training cost dominates, so the same choice is wrong in the
opposite direction. Our own runs, expressed the same way:

| run | bytes/param | BPE-token-equiv/param | Chinchilla D for that N | what we have seen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 95M @ 0.25 GB (measured) | 2.6 | 0.65 | 7.6 GB | **3.3%** |
| 95M @ 2.6 GB (Cerebras-matched) | 27 | 6.8 | 7.6 GB | 34% |
| 1B @ 22 GB (planned) | 22 | 5.5 | 80 GB | **28%** |
| 1B @ 80 GB (Chinchilla) | 80 | 20 | 80 GB | 100% |
| 65B @ 12T tokens (Ripple) | — | 185 | 1.3T tokens | 9.2× over |

So our 1B plan at 22 GB is **compute-misallocated by Chinchilla's own law**: for
5.5B BPE-tokens the optimal parameter count is ~275M, not 1B. Either train
275-300M on 22 GB, or train 1B on 80 GB. The patch unit is what makes the second
option possible on one card:

```
80 GB at 26.7 kB/s          = 832 h = 35 days
80 GB at 124.3 kB/s (patch) = 179 h = 7.5 days
```

That number — 7.5 days for a Chinchilla-optimal 1B — is the actual prize of the
patch path, and it is 4.6× cheaper than the same run on byte units.

## What is not transferable, stated plainly

- Their **3.3M GPU-hours and 12T tokens**: 23 years on our card. Any claim that
  we will match their capability is false; the claim we can defend is about
  quality *per FLOP* at ≤1e20.
- Their **40% MFU**: their problem is a 65B dense model on datacenter parts. Our
  13.9% MFU is a small-model launch-bound problem (kernel launch overhead
  dominates at 95M). Different bottleneck; their MFU number says nothing about
  our ceiling.
- Their **RL scale** (1.4M rollouts): 70× cheaper than the previous SOTA, but
  still far above what one card can do. The transferable part is the *method*
  (truncated IS, score centering, linear length penalty, synthetic environments),
  not the volume.

## Verdict

**VERIFIED (sourced):** the model exists as described, is dense, 65B/12T/1M, 40%
MFU, $32M; its ladder compounded to 16× on code CE at 4B; mHC was rejected at
4B; RL used 1.4M rollouts.

**VERIFIED (our repo):** we have none of the five cheap recipe knobs, and
`final_frac=0.0` is actively refused by `wsd_lr`.

**SPECULATION [S]:** their 16× includes data-mixture effects that will not
reproduce on our byte-level mix; expect 2-5× from the same knobs here.

**Action:** the next experiment budget goes to the recipe ladder (5 knobs ×
1-2 GPU-h each at 95M), not to a new architecture. Then the 1B decision is
275M@22GB vs 1B@80GB, made on the Chinchilla arithmetic above, not on ambition.
