## TL;DR (RU)

1. **«MoR cycle-2» — это не MoR.** Это строка `Vanilla 135M … Cyc 2` в Table 10 (Appendix F,
   p.29): обычный **Recursive Transformer** со схемой *Cycle*, **без роутера, без early-exit,
   без адаптивной глубины**. Все 30 проходов делает каждый токен. −0.0251 — это эффект
   **связывания весов** (15 уникальных слоёв × 2 прохода), а не эффект маршрутизации. Сам
   роутер в этой таблице не участвует; в iso-FLOP сетке (Table 9) на 135M он **проигрывает**
   vanilla на всех трёх бюджетах (+0.016 / +0.073 / +0.106 NLL) [T].
2. **Арифметика «53M из 106M» сходится точно.** 135M = 106.2M non-emb + 28.3M embedding;
   слой при d=576, f=1536, GQA 9/3×64 = 3 540 096 параметров; ×30 = 106.2M, ×15 = 53.1M.
   Уникальных параметров остаётся 81.4M (60.5% от 135M), а не 53M — embedding не шарится [C].
3. **Iso-FLOP — да, по построению.** Те же 10B токенов, та же ширина 576, та же
   эффективная глубина 30, те же гиперпараметры; Appendix прямо пишет: «Vanilla and Recursive
   Transformers have the same FLOPs» [T]. **Не** фиксированы: уникальные параметры (вдвое
   меньше), сид/дисперсия (в статье **нигде** нет seeds и error bars), KV-стратегия (не указана).
4. **В BPB это 0.009–0.012.** 0.0251 нат/токен ÷ (4.0 байт/токен × ln2) = 0.00905; относительно
   (0.83% от 3.03), перенесённое на наш 1.4906 = 0.0123. Наши шумы: 30 МБ 1σ≈0.02–0.03 → эффект
   0.3–0.6σ; 120 МБ 1σ≈0.009–0.011 (не измерен, интерполяция) → 0.8–1.3σ [M/C].
5. **Реплика: 6 уникальных слоёв × 2 прохода**, `block_form="transformer"`, `n_layers=6`,
   `max_iter=2`, d=832, f=3072. 47 731 008 параметров (50.2% контроля), **691.37 MFLOP/байт —
   ровно как у контроля**. Это и есть 15×2=30 в масштабе 6×2=12. Вариант «12 уникальных +
   второй проход по последним K» — **не** cycle-2: он либо 2× FLOPs (12×2), либо +K проходов
   сверх контроля, и параметров не экономит.
6. **Вердикт: НЕТ.** 1.5 ч — это не цена результата, это цена шума. По собственному протоколу
   репозитория нужно 3+3 сида (≈3.6 ч), и планка «больше разброса контроля по 3 сидам» ляжет
   ровно на величину эффекта. При этом приоритет литературы **против** реплики: 116-прогонный
   iso-depth закон даёт +0.03…+0.06 нат при r=2 [T], а единственная ячейка MoR без сидов
   переворачивает знак на 360M (+0.0016). Наиболее вероятный исход — не «−0.01», а «+0.01…+0.02».

## 0. The premise, corrected in one paragraph

Everything in the question's numbers is right: Table 10, 15 layers ×2, 53M of 106M, 3.0323 →
3.0071, iso-token and iso-FLOP. Three labels are wrong, and they change the decision:

* **It is not a MoR model.** Table 10 is Appendix F's ablation of *parameter-sharing strategies*
  for Recursive Transformers. The `Type` column of Table 3/9 (Expert / Token routing) does not
  exist in Table 10. There is no router, no per-token depth, no exit. The −0.0251 measures
  **weight tying at r=2**, which is the baseline MoR is built on top of, not MoR.
* **The authors do not recommend it.** §4.1: "Middle-Cycle is the most effective parameter
  sharing strategy … Based on these findings, we adopt the Middle-Cycle configuration for all
  subsequent MoR and Recursive Transformers." At 135M/r=2 Middle-Cycle is 3.0330 — a
  **+0.0007 tie with vanilla**. Cycle-2's −0.0251 is the one cell where Cycle is ahead [T].
* **"Three loops lose 0.0725" is the Middle-Cycle-3 row** (3.1048). The *same scheme as the
  replica*, Cycle-3 (10 unique ×3, 35M), is **3.1154 → +0.0831** [T, Table 10].

And the internal replication is missing from the summary: at **360M**, Cycle-2 (157M, 16
unique ×2) is **2.8487 vs vanilla 2.8471 → +0.0016, a loss**, while Middle-Cycle-2 wins
(−0.0176) [T, Table 10]. The effect does not survive a 2.7× parameter increase in the same
table, same data, same recipe.

## 1. The mechanism, from the paper

### 1.1 The four sharing schemes (Table 1 p.3; full list Table 5, App. A)

With a base stack of L layers and N_r recursions, the paper partitions the L layers into
N_r blocks of L/N_r layers each and reuses each block's weights:

| scheme | unrolled order for L=9, N_r=3 | unique layers | what is special |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cycle** | `[(0,1,2),(0,1,2),(0,1,2)]` | 3 | all layer positions share, cyclically |
| Sequence | `[(0,0,0),(1,1,1),(2,2,2)]` | 3 | each block repeated consecutively |
| Middle-Cycle | `[3, (0,1,2),(0,1,2), 3]`-shaped: unique first **and** last | L/N_r + 2 | first/last keep full capacity |
| Middle-Sequence | unique first/last, middle consecutive | L/N_r + 2 | worst of both, per the table |

Formally (Table 1): Cycle at layer ℓ uses `Φ′_{ℓ mod (L/N_r)}`; Middle-Cycle uses
`Φ′_{(ℓ−1 mod ((L−2)/N_r))+1}` for the middle, with `Φ_0` and `Φ_{L−1}` untouched.

### 1.2 Cycle-2, exactly

For the Table 10 row `Vanilla 135M | 53M | 15 | 10B | Cyc | 2`:

```
pass 1: layer 0, 1, ..., 14   (weights Φ'0..Φ'14)
pass 2: layer 0, 1, ..., 14   (the SAME Φ'0..Φ'14)
```

* 15 unique transformer layers, 2 passes, **30 layer-passes** = the vanilla 30L's depth.
* **Selection per cycle: none.** The order is deterministic `ℓ mod 15`. No router, no token
  drops out, no layer is skipped. Every token traverses all 30 passes.
* **Shared:** all 15 layer parameter sets — attention q/k/v/o, MLP gate/up/down, both RMSNorms.
  **Unique:** token embedding (28.3M), final norm, LM head (tied to the embedding in this config:
  106M + 28M = 134M ≈ 135M). There are **no per-depth parameters** — no iteration embedding, no
  per-depth norm gain — which is what makes the replica a pure tying test.
* **KV cache:** for a fixed-depth loop, every pass stores its own K/V, so the cache is that of a
  30-layer model. Table 10 does not say whether recursive sharing is on; if it were, memory would
  be 1/N_r = half, at some quality cost (Table 3: "Share" 2.7983 vs "Cache" 2.7925 at 360M/3).
  With no routing there is no recursion-wise-caching win at all — that mechanism's saving comes
  from tokens exiting.

### 1.3 The parameter arithmetic that gives 53M (Table 6 p.24)

135M base: `N_L=30`, `d_model=576`, `N_head=9`, `N_KV=3`, `d_head=64`, `d_inter=1536`,
vocab 49K, ctx 2K, N-emb 106M, Emb 28M.

| component | count | ×30 layers |
|---|---:|---:|
| q = o = 576×576 | 331 776 each | |
| k = v = 576×(3×64) | 110 592 each | |
| SwiGLU MLP = 3×576×1536 | 2 654 208 | |
| 2 RMSNorm = 2×576 | 1 152 | |
| **per layer** | **3 540 096** | **106 202 880 ≈ 106M** |
| **15 unique** | | **53 101 440 ≈ 53M** |
| embedding 49 152×576 | 28 311 552 | 28M |
| **unique total** | | **81 412 992 = 60.5% of 135M** |

So "53M kept" is exactly half the **non-embedding** parameters, and the model that remains is
**81M unique**, not 53M. The saving is 39.5% of the weights, not 50%. (Optimizer state and
FLOPs: FLOPs unchanged; optimizer state halves with non-emb params.)

### 1.4 The routing / exit rule — and why it is not in this table

The two routers are defined in §2.2.1 (p.4) and used in Tables 3/7/9, **not** in Table 10:

* **Expert-choice** (Eq. 2.1): at recursion r a router scores every still-active token,
  `g = G(θ_rᵀ H_r)`, keeps the top-k by the β-percentile, and only those continue
  ("hierarchical filtering": a token not selected at r cannot be re-evaluated at r+1).
  Capacity is sequenced `N_r/N_r, …, 1/N_r`, so step 1 sees all tokens, step N_r sees `1/N_r`.
* **Token-choice** (Eq. 2.2): one top-1 decision over N_r "experts" from the first hidden state,
  committed for the whole forward; the hidden state is updated only at the chosen depth.

What the paper measures for them at 135M is a **loss**, at every iso-FLOP budget
(Table 9 p.27): 2.0e18 3.1077 vs 3.0922; 5.0e18 3.0192 vs 2.9464; 16.5e18 2.9490 vs 2.8432.
Routing crosses over at **360M** (2.0e18: 3.1026 vs 3.3785, −0.276). So the router is neither
part of cycle-2 nor a candidate at our scale.

### 1.5 KV-cache implications, in one line

Training: none (our trainer has no cache; the 30 passes are ordinary activations, so activation
memory equals a 30-layer model's — the loop wins zero training memory). Inference at fixed
depth: 30 layer-slots of KV, same as the 30L control; only *recursive sharing* would halve it,
and that is a separate switch not exercised by Table 10.

## 2. The iso-FLOP comparison: what was fixed, what was not

### 2.1 The rows that matter (Table 10 p.29, all 10B tokens, identical hyperparameters)

Passes = unique × N_r, plus the two unshared layers for the Middle variants. Note that
Middle-Cycle-3 is **29** passes, not 30 (1+9×3+1), i.e. 3% fewer FLOPs than the 30L — the paper
does not pad Table 10 to exact iso-FLOP, so its r=3 Middle row is a ~3%-cheaper comparison.

| row | unique non-emb | N_L | passes | NLL | Δ vs 30L vanilla |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Vanilla 30L (FLOP twin) | 106M | 30 | 30 | **3.0323** | — |
| Vanilla 15L (param twin) | 53M | 15 | 15 | 3.0818 | +0.0495 |
| **Cycle-2 (15×2)** | **53M** | **15** | **30** | **3.0071** | **−0.0251** |
| Middle-Cycle-2 (1+14+1) | 57M | 16 | 30 | 3.0330 | +0.0007 |
| Cycle-3 (10×3) | 35M | 10 | 30 | 3.1154 | +0.0831 |
| Middle-Cycle-3 (1+9+1) | 39M | 11 | 29 | 3.1048 | +0.0725 |
| Cycle-2 @360M (16×2) | 157M | 16 | 32 | 2.8487 | +0.0016 (vs 32L 2.8471) |

**Fixed:** 10B tokens, FineWeb-Edu, tokenizer/vocab, width (576), sequence length (2K),
effective depth (30), optimizer/LR/schedule — and therefore training FLOPs by construction
(Appendix: "Vanilla and Recursive Transformers have the same FLOPs").
**Not fixed:** unique parameters (halved); **seed/variance** — the paper reports no seeds,
no error bars and no ± anywhere in 30 pages (verified by full-text search) `[T: absence]`;
KV strategy (unstated); inference memory (unchanged at fixed depth).

It is worth naming what the −0.0251 is *relative to what*:

* vs its **FLOP twin** (106M/30L): −0.0251 — the headline.
* vs its **param twin** (53M/15L): **−0.0747** — the robust part.
* At 360M the FLOP-twin comparison flips to **+0.0016**, while the param-twin comparison stays
  −0.042. Read together, the consistent claim is: *at r=2, depth reuse recovers most of the loss
  from halving the parameters; at 135M it overshoots by 0.025, at 360M it undershoots by 0.002.*
  The overshoot is one cell, not a mechanism.

### 2.2 The BPB equivalent of −0.0251

NLL is nats per **token** (Llama/SmolLM 49K tokenizer). The paper never states bytes/token; on
FineWeb-Edu English prose 3.5–4.5 bytes/token is the plausible band, centred at 4.0 `[S]`.

* **Absolute transfer** (same nats per byte of text):
  `0.0251 / (4.0 × ln2) = 0.00905 BPB` — band **0.0080–0.0103** for 3.5–4.5 B/token `[C]`.
* **Relative transfer** (same fractional loss): `1.4906 × 0.0251/3.0323 = 0.01234 BPB` `[C]`.
  (Their own loss is ≈1.09 BPB, so the two conversions agree to ≈0.009 — the metric is not the
  fragile part of this.)

**Expected win: 0.009–0.012 BPB.** For comparison, the *robust* row (vs the 15L param twin)
would be 0.027–0.037 BPB — but that is not the control we would run against.

### 2.3 Against our noise floors

| budget | 1σ | source | effect 0.010 is |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 30 MB | 0.02–0.03 | interpolation; nearest measured 0.051 @36.9 MB `[M]`/`[C]` | **0.3–0.6σ** |
| 73.7 MB | 0.011 | 3 arms × 2 seeds `[M]` | 0.9σ |
| **120 MB** | **0.009–0.011** | not measured; 1/√D from 73.7 MB `[C]` | **0.9–1.1σ** |
| 1.9 GB | ~0.0021 | 1/√D, unverified over 26× `[C]` | 4.7σ |

Power, using σ_arm = 0.011 and two independent single-seed arms (σ_Δ = 0.0156):

* pass bar 0.020 (the repo's own ladder rule): **10% false-positive rate**, **26% power** at the
  paper's Δ=0.010. Three of four real wins are missed, and one null in ten is called a win.
* To resolve 0.010 at 2σ you need σ_Δ ≤ 0.005 → **≈10 seeds per arm** (12 h at 120 MB). That is
  the honest price, and it is 8× the 1.5 h on the table.

**So: the expected win is not visible at 30 MB (0.3–0.6σ), is at ~1σ at 120 MB, and is only
decidable at ~2 GB — where it costs 18.8 h for two arms.**

### 2.4 The contradicting, better-powered evidence

The iso-depth scaling law (arXiv:2604.21106v3, §4.2, read from the PDF) fits **116 runs**,
20 effective layers, r∈{1,2,4,8}, six budgets, and reports a **95% CI** on φ=0.46 [0.41,0.53].
Its r=2 loss frontier **trails** the non-looped baseline by **+0.03…+0.06 nats** across the grid,
with the gap *widening* at lower budgets. That is the same experiment as Table 10's Cycle-2
(same width, same total passes, half the unique params, same FineWeb-Edu) and it has the
**opposite sign**. Expressed in BPB: **+0.011…+0.022**.

Two experiments that close, disagreeing by 0.055–0.085 nats, and only one of them reports
variance. The rational prior is the one with 116 runs and a CI, i.e. **the replica is expected to
lose by ~0.01–0.02 BPB**, not win by 0.01.

## 3. The replica on our stack

### 3.1 Which flags map to what

| paper concept | our field | today's semantics | for a Cycle-2 replica |
|---|---|---|---|
| `N_r` (passes) | `max_iter` | kda: passes over the loop; **transformer: refused ≠1** (`config.py:3246`) | **becomes the pass count** on the transformer form |
| `L_rec` (unique recurrent layers) | `n_layers` (transformer) / `loop_blocks` (kda) | number of distinct bodies | **6** (12 passes / 2) |
| Cycle vs Middle-Cycle | `prelude_layers`/`coda_layers` | 0/0 = Cycle; kda-only, and `UniqueLayer` is a **half-width FFN**, not a transformer block | **0/0 = Cycle** — the only faithful half. Middle-Cycle is *not* transcribable without building full unique blocks (`prelude.py:41-78`) |
| block form | `block_form` | `"transformer"` = the control's block | **`"transformer"`** — using `"kda"` would change block *and* tying, two variables |
| router / exit | `mod_capacity` (MoD) is per-token *dropping*, not depth routing | — | **not used**: Table 10 has no router, and Table 9 says routing loses at 135M |

On the KDA form the paper's topology is already expressible — `loop_blocks=6, max_iter=12`
runs bodies 0..5 twice (the config comment at `config.py:508-514` says exactly
`blocks[i % loop_blocks]`). But that arm loops the KDA/Engram/MoE block, so it is not comparable
to the dense control. The faithful A/B needs the dense form to loop, which it cannot today.

### 3.2 Why 6×2, and not the other shapes

| candidate replica | passes | total params | FLOPs vs control | is it cycle-2? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **6 unique × 2** | **12** | **47.7M (50%)** | **1.00×** | **yes — 15×2=30 scaled down** |
| 12 unique × 2 | 24 | 95.0M (100%) | 2.00× | no — 2× compute, no param saving |
| 12 unique + 2nd pass over last K | 12+K | 95.0M (100%) | 1+K/12× | no — a tail loop, not cycle sharing |
| 4 unique × 3 | 12 | 32.0M (34%) | 1.00× | yes at r=3; the paper's r=3 rows **lose** 0.083 |
| Middle-Cycle-2 (1+5×2+1) | 12 | 55.2M (58%) | 1.00× | yes, but needs full unique prelude/coda blocks |

The "12 unique + revisit the last K" shape the question suggests is a different mechanism with a
different bill: it *keeps* full parameter count and *adds* compute, so it is strictly worse than
the control on both axes under the paper's own accounting. It also is not what any row of Table 10
measures. Reject it.

### 3.3 The minimal code change (~10 lines, 4 files + tests)

Today the transformer form builds `n_layers` distinct blocks and runs each **once**
(`model.py:316-321`, `model.py:1368-1372`), and `config.py:3246` refuses `max_iter != 1`.
The change is to make `max_iter` mean "passes" on that form:

```python
# config.py:3246  — replace the refusal
if model.max_iter < 1:
    loud(f"max_iter={model.max_iter}: a pass count is >= 1 ... Escape: max_iter >= 1.")

# model.py:316  — distinct bodies vs passes
n_layers = cfg.n_layers or cfg.loop_blocks          # bodies (was loop_blocks * max_iter)
passes   = cfg.max_iter                             # cycle count
self.transformer_passes = passes
self.transformer_blocks = nn.ModuleList([
    # init std / residual scale keep the EFFECTIVE depth: n_layers*passes.
    TransformerBlock(cfg, n_layers=n_layers * passes, layer_idx=i, device=device)
    for i in range(n_layers)
])

# model.py:1368  — cycle the stack
seq = list(self.transformer_blocks) * self.transformer_passes
for block in stepaudit.iterations("forward.transformer_blocks", seq):
    ...

# mod_ledger.py:131  — price PASSES, not bodies
layers = (cfg.n_layers or cfg.loop_blocks) * cfg.max_iter
```

Plus: refuse `byte_patch & max_iter > 1` (that branch also walks the stack once, `model.py:965`),
and update `tests/test_block_form.py:146` (drop the `max_iter: 2` refusal case; add pins:
`n_layers=6, max_iter=2` → 47 731 008 params and 12 block calls; `n_layers=0, max_iter=1` → the
old 1-body behaviour; the control `n_layers=12, max_iter=1` is bit-identical).
`n_layers * passes` for the init is the load-bearing detail: `TransformerBlock` draws
`0.02/√(2·n_layers)` (`transformer.py:201-202`), so building 6 bodies with `n_layers=6` would
inflate every output projection by √2 versus the control — a confound, not a replica.

### 3.4 Parameter and FLOP arithmetic

| | control | replica | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| block params | 94 608 384 (12×7 884 032) | 47 304 192 (6×7 884 032) | 50.0% |
| embed + norm + head | 426 816 | 426 816 | — |
| **total** | **95 035 200** | **47 731 008** | **50.2%** |
| non-emb (blocks+norm) | 94 609 216 | 47 305 024 | 50.0% |
| layer-passes | 12 | 12 | 100% |
| MFLOP/byte | 691.37 | **691.37** | **100%** |

The FLOP identity is exact, not approximate: forward = `2·L_eff·(4d²+2d·f) + 4·L_eff·T·d + 2dV`
with `L_eff = 12` in both arms, d=832, f=3072, T=1024, V=256 → 230.457 MFLOP/byte forward →
**691.372 fwd+bwd** `[C]`, which is the config's printed 691.37. At 120 MB that is
**8.30e16 FLOPs and 35.6 min per arm** at the measured 56 269 B/s.

### 3.5 What the replica is not

* **Not Middle-Cycle.** The paper recommends it, and at 135M it is a tie (3.0330). Our
  `prelude_layers`/`coda_layers` are half-width FFN-only layers, so using them would not
  transcribe MoR's unique first/last transformer layers. Building that is a bigger change for a
  configuration the paper measured as +0.0007.
* **Not MoR.** No router, no adaptive depth, no KV saving.
* **Not deep supervision.** The transformer form's `_forward_transformer` takes one final CE
  (`model.py:1350-1384`) — same as the paper. The KDA loop form's `L_Rec` per-iteration CE would
  have been an extra confound; it is not in this path.
* **Not the same data/param regime.** The paper: 10B tokens ≈ 40 GB of text on 81.4M unique
  params ≈ **492 bytes/param**. Ours: 120 MB on 95.0M ≈ **1.26 bytes/param** — **~390× less**.
  (In tokens: 123 vs 1.26 tokens/param, ~100×.) Their point is at or past the usual ~20
  tokens/param compute-optimal mark, i.e. the regime where extra unique parameters have stopped
  buying much loss — which is exactly where halving them can be free. We are 16× *below* that
  mark: our control still fits its 120 MB (0.981 nats train vs 1.4906 BPB eval, i.e. a 0.075 BPB
  train/held-out gap, `control_refetch.jsonl` final step), so its parameters are still being used.

## 4. Pre-registered A/B

### 4.1 Arms

Budget 120 MB = 14 648 steps × b8 × s1024, `mix_refetch`, `doc_masking=false`, lr 3e-4,
`opt=mix`, `muon_lr_mult=12`, FAST=1, seed 1, eval `best/fwedu_eval.bin[0:196608]`, metric
**`bpb_fin`** (never `bpb_mean`).

| arm | config | params | MFLOP/B | what it answers |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **C** | `configs/flagship3_dense.toml` (12×1) | 95 035 200 | 691.37 | the control, re-run so the pair is same-day/same-card |
| **R** | same + `--set n_layers=6 --set max_iter=2` (6×2) | 47 731 008 | 691.37 | the replica |
| (D) | same + `--set n_layers=6 --set max_iter=1` (6×1) | 47 731 008 | 345.7 | *optional third arm*: the param-twin comparison the paper's −0.0747 belongs to |

Arm D is the only one with a **visible** predicted effect (R − D ≈ −0.03 BPB, 2–3σ), but it is
guaranteed to favour R for a trivial reason: R gets 2× the FLOPs. Run it only if the goal is to
demonstrate the mechanism is live, not to decide anything about the 2 GB recipe.

### 4.2 Decision rule (pre-registered, before any number is seen)

Let Δ = BPB(R) − BPB(C); negative = replica better.

1. **Re-run C.** Do not reuse 1.4906: a cross-day pair is not a pair.
2. Seed 1:
   * **Δ₁ ≥ +0.010** → close the lane: tying loses at this scale.
   * **|Δ₁| < 0.010** → close the lane as "below resolution". **Do not buy seed 2** — two seeds
     give σ_mean = 0.0078, a 95% CI of ±0.015 around a 0.005 effect; the second seed cannot
     resolve what the first could not.
   * **−0.030 < Δ₁ ≤ −0.010** → run seed 2 on both arms. Win iff the 2-seed mean ≤ −0.015 **and**
     both seeds are negative.
   * **Δ₁ ≤ −0.030** → run seed 2 on both arms. Win iff the mean ≤ −0.020 and both negative.
3. A win at 120 MB earns a **1.9 GB confirm** (2 arms × 9.4 h), not adoption. A tie or a loss
   closes the loop-tying lane at this scale.

Note this is deliberately the *opposite* of "second seed if the first is within 0.01": at
|Δ₁|<0.01 you have the least information per seed, and the extra seed still cannot reach 2σ at
0.01. The seed belongs on the *candidate*, not on the null.

If the repo's own AB-PROTOCOL is applied literally ("an arm wins if its mean is below the
control's mean by more than the spread across the control's own 3 seeds"), the run is **3+3
seeds** — and the control has **no 3-seed spread at 120 MB today** (one run, 1.4906). The bar
would then be that measured spread, likely 0.010–0.015, i.e. the effect itself.

### 4.3 Power, stated before the run

* σ_arm = 0.011 (73.7 MB, between seeds; 120 MB unmeasured).
* Two arms, one seed: σ_Δ = 0.0156 → at a 0.020 bar, **10% false positive, 26% power** at the
  paper's Δ=0.010. Under the φ law's Δ=+0.015, power ≈ 3%.
* 3+3 seeds: σ_Δ = 0.009 → 2σ = 0.018 > 0.010. Still underpowered; power at Δ=0.010 ≈ 13%
  against a 2σ bar.
* The only budget where the effect is comfortably visible is ~1.9 GB (0.010 ≈ 4.7σ).

### 4.4 Cost

| item | card-hours |
|---|---:|
| 2 arms × 35.6 min (seed 1) + eval/startup | **~1.3 h** |
| + seed 2 pair if triggered | +1.3 h → 2.6 h |
| 3+3 seeds (repo protocol) | **~3.8 h** |
| 1.9 GB confirm (2 arms) | **18.8 h** |
| CPU engineering (diff + 4 test pins + ledger) | 0 card-h, ~1 h agent |

Expected cost of the 1.5 h plan: 1.3 h + P(trigger)×1.3 h ≈ 1.9 h, with an expected outcome of
"tie below resolution".

### 4.5 What a win would and would not mean

A win at 120 MB (≥0.02) means: at 1.26 bytes/param, 6 unique layers applied twice match 12 unique
layers at the same FLOPs, with half the weights. That is a **deployment** fact (weights and
optimizer state halve), not a compute fact. It would not change the 2 GB training run, whose
decision space is data and recipe; it would justify a 18.8 h confirm at 1.9 GB and, if that holds,
a parameter-efficiency claim for the 1B-on-one-card story. A tie means nothing changes. A loss
closes the lane.

## 7. SPECULATION (my inference)

* **[S1] bytes/token = 4.0** for FineWeb-Edu under the SmolLM 49K tokenizer. The paper does not
  state it. The 3.5–4.5 band gives 0.0080–0.0103 BPB; the conclusion (≈1σ at 120 MB) is
  insensitive to the choice.
* **[S2] The two literatures' disagreement (MoR −0.025 vs iso-depth +0.03…+0.06 nats at r=2) is
  not resolvable from the papers**: they differ in recurrent-block depth (15 vs 4–16), data
  (10B tokens vs 6 budgets of FineWeb-Edu), and one has no seed spread. My prior is the
  better-powered one, but this is a judgement, not a measurement.
* **[S3] Regime transfer is the biggest unknown.** At 1.26 bytes/param our control is still
  fitting (0.075 BPB train/held-out gap); halving its unique parameters is not the free
  regularizer it is at 492 bytes/param. It could cost more than the paper's −0.01, or the
  depth-reuse could be worth more because the model is nowhere near its capacity ceiling.
  I assume a small loss, with a band of ±0.02.
* **[S4] The 6-layer recurrent block is 2.5× fatter (7.88M params) than MoR's 15-layer block
  (3.54M)** and our aspect ratio is 12 layers at d=832 vs 30 at d=576. No published measurement
  covers this shape; the φ exponent was fit on 4–16-layer blocks at 20 effective layers.
* **[S5] A paired same-seed, same-data σ for two architectures at 120 MB might be smaller than
  0.011** (the 0.011 is pooled across arms and seeds; the same-config repeat RMS is 0.0048).
  If it is 0.005, two seeds per arm would suffice. The repo has not measured it, and measuring it
  costs as much as the experiment — which is itself an argument against this being the test to
  spend the card on.

## 8. Open questions

1. **What is the across-architecture paired σ at 120 MB?** 2 seeds of the control and 2 of the
   replica would give a 2-point estimate per arm — not enough. This is the missing instrument,
   and it is worth measuring once on a cheap pair (e.g. `vr` vs control) before any 0.01-scale
   claim.
2. **Does the tying effect change sign with bytes/param?** A 3-point curve (6×2 vs 12×1 at 120 MB,
   600 MB, 1.9 GB) would answer it, at ~1 + 3 + 9.4 h. That is the experiment that would tell us
   whether the paper's regime is reachable at our scale — and it is 10× the cost of the screening
   test, which is why the screening test is the wrong first step.
3. **Would Middle-Cycle on *full* blocks beat Cycle at our shape?** The paper says yes at 360M
   (2.8295 vs 2.8487) and by 0.0007 at 135M. Untestable today: our prelude/coda are half-width
   FFN layers, not transformer blocks. Building full unique blocks is ~3× the diff for a
   +0.0007 prior.
4. **Is the 1-layer × 3 loop form even the same family?** No — `2026-11-06-depth-recurrence.md`
   already answered this: 1 unique layer is 8% of the control's depth and sits outside every
   published fit. The replica (6×2) is the *first* loop shape at this stack that the literature
   actually covers; that is its only merit.
