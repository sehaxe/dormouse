# Small-LM training dynamics: held-out at uniform while train collapses (research pass, 2026-09-26)

**Our measured situation (given, not re-derived):** 7.5M-param byte LM (vocab 256), KDA + 24M-row hashed n-gram Engram + low-rank experts, Muon+, peak lr 1e-4, batch 10×512 = 5,120 bytes/step, online over 20 GB (each byte once, zero repeats). Train CE → 0.1–0.4 within 200–600 steps (= 1.0–3.1 MB seen); held-out CE ~5.5 nats = 7.9–8.0 BPB; one variant froze at exactly uniform. Uniform over 256 is ln 256 = 5.545 nats = 8.000 bits/byte (arithmetic), so held-out moved ≤ 0.1 bits.

**Method:** primary sources only (arXiv HTML/abs pages, authors' repos, Shannon 1951 via Bell System TJ PDF); every number below was read off the cited source. Negative claims ("term X absent") were verified by grep of the fetched full text. Where literature has no direct answer, that is said explicitly.

---

## 1. When should held-out start moving? Data-seen thresholds for a 7.5M-param model

**Chinchilla budget.** Hoffmann et al. trained 400+ models (70M–16B params, 5–500B tokens) and found compute-optimal training scales params and tokens *equally* — N_opt ∝ C^0.50, D_opt ∝ C^0.50 (approach 1), a≈0.46/b≈0.54 (parametric fit) [1]. Their projected table: 400M→8.0B tokens, 1B→20.2B, 10B→205.1B, 70B→1.5T — a stable **≈20 tokens/param** across two orders of magnitude; Chinchilla itself is 70B/1.4T [1]. For 7.5M params this implies ≈150M tokens ≈ **150 MB** (1 byte = 1 token). Our 200–600-step diagnosis window (1.0–3.1 MB) is **0.7–2% of that budget**; 150 MB ≈ 29,300 steps at 5,120 bytes/step (arithmetic). Caveat: Chinchilla's tokens are BPE (mC4 averages 4.14 bytes/token [2]), so a byte-level model's per-byte loss target differs; treat 150 MB as an order-of-magnitude re-judge point, not a fitted number. The loss functional form L(N,D)=E+A/N^α+B/D^β is their fit [1]; its constants were not re-derived here.

**Byte/char-model evidence points the same way, slower:**
- **ByT5** [2]: byte models are data-inefficient per unit text — at a fixed token budget a byte model sees "roughly 4× less actual text" (mC4 compression 4.14 bytes/token), pays ~1.2× the einsum ops, and is nevertheless competitive "despite being pre-trained on 4× less text". Byte-level ≠ doomed, just hungrier.
- **bGPT** [3]: next-byte prediction matches specialized models across text/audio/image (ABC→MIDI conversion error 0.0011 bits/byte; CPU-execution simulation >99.99% accuracy) — byte LMs work given data+compute.
- **nanoGPT shakespeare-char** [4]: a 6-layer/384-dim (~10.7M-param) char GPT, batch 64×256 chars, 5,000 iters = **81.9M chars (~74 epochs** of the ~1 MB corpus), lr 1e-3 → best val loss **1.4697** (uniform over its 65-char vocab is ln 65 ≈ 4.17, arithmetic). Note it gets there with massive repetition, not single-pass.
- **TinyStories** [5]: models **below 10M total params** produce fluent, consistent multi-paragraph stories — on a narrow synthetic corpus with many epochs.
- **No direct source found** for "held-out crosses below uniform after X bytes of single-pass web text" — no published curve isolates that threshold. Closest anchors: the Chinchilla budget above, and Shannon's n-gram entropies for printed English (26-letter): F0=4.70, **F1=4.14, F2=3.56, F3=3.3** bits/letter [6]. These give a hard floor for diagnosis: a model that has learned even *unigram* frequencies scores ~4.1–4.7 bits/byte-ish on English text; uniform is 8.0. **Our held-out at 7.9–8.0 BPB is above a unigram baseline** — the deployed predictor transmits ~zero information about the next byte on unseen data. That is not "slow convergence"; that is "the non-memory part of the blend is uninformative".

## 2. Memorize-then-generalize (grokking and friends)

- **Power et al.** [7]: on small algorithmic datasets, networks "improv[e] generalization performance from random chance level to perfect generalization... well past the point of overfitting", and — the load-bearing sentence for us — "**smaller datasets require increasing amounts of optimization for generalization**". Held-out sitting at chance (= uniform prediction) for a long horizon while train is perfect is *the documented regime* for small data.
- **Omnigrok** [8]: grokking = train/test loss mismatch vs weight norm ("LU mechanism"); it "can nicely explain many aspects of grokking: **data size dependence, weight decay dependence**"; grokking can be *induced* on "images, language and molecules" and eliminated on algorithmic data by norm surgery. Governing knobs in the literature: data size (more data → earlier/abrupt transition disappears), weight decay (bounds norm → speeds generalization), plus dropout/regularization.
- **kNN-LM's memorizing-LM experiment** [9] is the closest published twin of our symptom: a 247M Transformer trained **without dropout** "eventually reaches zero training loss" — full memorization — while its validation perplexity is 28.59 vs 17.96 for the dropout-regularized model; and interpolating the memorizer back in recovers only 0.1 ppl. Memorizing capacity, once used, *does not* convert into generalizing representations on its own.
- **ST-MoE** [10] shows the same shape for memory-augmented (sparse) models: on a 250-example task the sparse model races to 100% train accuracy while lagging its dense twin on validation — over-parameterized/memory-heavy models are the first to overfit small data.

What governs the transition, per the above: unique-data size (dominant), weight decay / norm control, regularization (dropout), and total optimization steps. LR changes the clock speed, not the mechanism.

## 3. Repeats / replay ratio

- **"Replay ratio" is not a DeepSeek/Muon-pretraining term.** We grepped the full text of Moonlight (arXiv:2502.16982): the word does not occur [11]. The term belongs to deep RL ("replay ratio (update-to-data ratio, UTD)" [12]) and frontier-lab practice blogs. No primary pretraining paper we opened prescribes a "replay ratio ~2–4×".
- **The canonical repeats result** is Muennighoff et al., Scaling Data-Constrained LMs [13]: "training with **up to 4 epochs of repeated data yields negligible changes to loss** compared to having unique data. However, with more repetition, the value of adding compute eventually decays to zero." So ≤4× repetition is approximately free; >4× decays.
- **But the premise of the token-crisis papers does not hold for us.** Repeats buy loss when unique data is scarce relative to the compute-optimal budget. Our compute-optimal budget is ~150 MB [1] and we hold 20 GB unique (~130× surplus). Zero repeats is the *correct* default for the core; the binding constraint is elsewhere (§5, §6).
- Nuance worth keeping: nanoGPT-char reaches sub-uniform quickly *because of* ~74 epochs over 1 MB [4], and TinyStories' tiny models need epochs [5]. A short "warmup subset" re-read a few times at the very start is a legitimate accelerant for the core — permitted by [13] (≤4 epochs is free) — but it is an optimization, not a fix for held-out ≈ uniform.

## 4. Uniform / entropy collapse: symptoms and standard cures

Documented collapse family (all about degenerate distributions during training):
- **Attention-entropy collapse** [14]: pathologically low attention entropy accompanies oscillating loss/divergence; a tight lower bound on attention entropy "decreases exponentially fast with the spectral norm of the attention logits"; cure = σReparam (spectral normalization + learned scalar on linear layers).
- **Softmax saturation** [15]: QK-norm — ℓ2-normalize queries and keys per head, scale by a learnable parameter — makes softmax "less prone to arbitrary saturation".
- **Logit blowup / roundoff-driven instability** [10]: the **z-loss** L_z = (1/B)·Σ(log Σ_j e^{x_j})² penalizes large logits into an exponential; ST-MoE uses coefficient **c_z = 0.001** and notes they "also experiment[ed] with adding z-losses onto the **attention logits** which also improves model instability without hurting quality". PaLM-class models adopted the final-logits z-loss on the same principle (per [10]'s framing of the z-loss lineage; we opened ST-MoE, and cite PaLM only as background, not for numbers).

**Does any paper describe held-out collapsing to EXACTLY uniform while train memorizes? — No direct source found.** The nearest documented states: (a) grokking's test accuracy at chance level while train is perfect [7] — chance prediction over K classes is exactly the uniform predictor; (b) the memorizing Transformer whose val loss stalls high while train → 0 [9]. One mechanical observation of ours (arithmetic, not literature): CE *exactly* at ln 256 means the mean information in the predictive distribution is ~0 — consistent with near-constant logits on unseen inputs; if the Engram's collision rows (random init, never updated for unseen contexts) dominated the blend, you would expect held-out slightly *worse* than uniform, not cleaner — so "exactly uniform" points to an uninformative-but-benign core output, or heavy averaging/normalization washing logits toward 0, rather than noise injection.

Standard cures, ranked by evidence: z-loss 1e-3 on final logits (and optionally attention logits) [10]; QK-norm [15]; σReparam/entropy monitoring [14]. All three target the same quantity family: logit magnitude / distributional entropy.

## 5. Muon lr for a ~10M model: is 1e-4 peak reasonable?

Published anchors, with their update-scaling conventions (lr numbers are **not comparable across conventions** — check which one burn-muon-plus implements before transferring):
- **Moonlight / "Muon is Scalable"** [11]: they rescale the Muon update to match AdamW's update RMS — W ← W − η(0.2·O_t·√max(A,B) + λW) — explicitly so that "**Muon can directly reuse the learning rate and weight decay tuned for AdamW**" (AdamW update RMS empirically 0.2–0.4). Their AdamW-tuned LRs from the scaling-law grid: **9.503e-4 (399M), 9.143e-4 (545M), 8.825e-4 (822M), 8.561e-4 (1.1B), 8.305e-4 (1.5B)**, batch 96–256 seqs × 8K tokens; the production 16B MoE runs peak lr **4.2e-4** (linear warmup 2k steps), batch 2048→4096 seqs × 8K, wd 0.1. LR trends *down* with scale, so a 7.5M model in this convention lands at ≥1e-3-class.
- **Keller Jordan's Muon repo** (author's notes) [16]: hidden weights get **lr=0.02** with the original √max(1,A/B) update scaling, while embeddings/head/1-D params get AdamW at 3e-4; "only the learning rate and weight decay must be tuned"; the lr "should have built-in muP scaling". The two conventions differ by the 0.2·√d factor [11].
- **Batch pairing:** Moonlight's Muon runs pair lr ~4e-4–1e-3 with 0.8M–33.5M tokens/batch [11]; Keller's GPT-2-scale records pair 0.02 with ~0.5M tokens/batch [16]. Our batch is 5,120 bytes — at the *small* end, which if anything tolerates *larger* per-step lr, not smaller.
- **Verdict on 1e-4:** it is below every published anchor in either convention — ≥4× below Moonlight's 16B value (and ~10× below the ≥1e-3 that their size-LR trend implies for a 7.5M model), ~200× below the Keller-convention value. **Too low is the likely direction** (the hypothesis said "wrong" without direction). Diagnostic before re-tuning, straight from [11]: log per-group **update RMS**; if the Muon groups are far below the AdamW groups' 0.2–0.4, the effective lr is starved. Caveat we cannot resolve from public sources: Muon+'s ColRow normalization (our vendored build) may already fold in a shape-dependent scale — verify against its own docs, then sweep ~3e-4→3e-3 in the RMS-matched convention. Also note Chinchilla's practice of matching the LR-schedule horizon to the token budget [1].

## 6. Neural core + n-gram/lookup memory trained jointly: keeping the memory in its lane

The classical anchor for combining a lookup/statistical component with a learned model is interpolation (Jelinek–Mercer-style mixture; modern form in [9]): p = λ·p_memory + (1−λ)·p_core with **λ fixed and small**. The literature's recurring design choice is: **the memory never gets to dominate the training signal**.

- **kNN-LM** [9]: the neural LM is trained *alone*; the datastore is bolted on afterwards, interpolated at **λ = 0.25** on Wikitext-103 (18.65 → 16.12 test ppl; 15.79 with a continuous cache added), λ rising to 0.65 only for out-of-domain adaptation. λ is **tuned on held-out data**, not learned from train CE. Two more results matter for us: (i) surface-form **n-gram interpolation adds almost nothing** (0.2 ppl) at 247M-param scale — the value of lookup memory must be re-validated as the core grows; (ii) the memorizing-LM experiment (§2) shows parameter-memorization actively *degrades* the shared representations.
- **Bakhtin et al., adaptive neural+n-gram mixture** [17]: the mixture weight is predicted by "a small network" **trained quickly on small amounts of held-out data** — explicitly *not* the training-loss gradient of the main model — and beats static ensembles on One Billion Word. Gating exists, but it is regularized and data-selected, not free end-to-end parameters chasing train CE.
- **SCONE (Scaling Embedding Layers)** [18]: frequent n-gram embeddings are "**learned with a separate model during training**", then precomputed and served from off-accelerator memory; the n-gram side enters as *input features*, never as a rival output head sharing the CE gradient. Scaling both n-gram count and the learner makes a 1B-accelerator-resident model beat a 1.9B baseline at ~half inference FLOPs/memory — the strongest published validation of dormouse's off-accelerator n-gram bet.
- **ST-MoE** [10]: for the memory-augmented (sparse) family, small-data overfitting is expected; expert-hidden **dropout** helps modestly; fine-tuning hyperparameters need their own sensitivity discipline.

**No paper found** that jointly trains a hashed n-gram table on the same online stream it is evaluated against, i.e. exactly our Engram setup; none therefore documents "lookup memory monopolizes the gradient early" verbatim. The design pattern the literature repeats instead: keep the memory a **minority, separately-fitted component** (small fixed λ [9]; held-out-tuned gate [17]; separate feature model [18]) — precisely so that train CE keeps measuring the *core*, and the memory's contribution is judged on held-out data.

## Verdicts for dormouse

**V1 — Re-judge at 100–150 MB: right scale, wrong reason to expect uniform now. (Hypothesis (i): half-survives.)**
(a) Our numbers: 200–600 steps = 1.0–3.1 MB seen; held-out 7.9–8.0 BPB ≈ 8.0 uniform. (b) Chinchilla's ≈20 tokens/param gives ≈150 MB for 7.5M params [1] — hypothesis (i)'s re-judge point is exactly the compute-optimal budget; grokking's "smaller datasets require increasing optimization" [7] says held-out at chance at 1–3 MB is *possible*. **But** "expected" is too generous: even a unigram model scores ~4.1–4.7 bits [6], so held-out ≈ uniform means the core's *contribution to the blend is uninformative*, which no data-budget argument excuses. Verdict: keep the 150 MB (≈29k steps) re-judge date, but do not wait passively — fix V2/V3 first, and demand held-out < 6 BPB (below-unigram territory) within the first tens of MB as the sanity bar for the core.

**V2 — The Engram is monopolizing the objective; gate/warmup/separate it. (Hypothesis (ii): survives, best-fit explanation, with a mechanism caveat.)**
(a) Our numbers: train CE 0.1–0.4 nats = 0.14–0.58 bits/byte, which is 6–20× *below* Shannon's trigram entropy F3 = 3.3 bits/letter [6] — train CE at that level cannot be n-gram statistics; it is row-level lookup of contexts the Engram was just updated on (online train/eval leakage). Held-out ≈ uniform says the blend routes ~no core signal to unseen data. (b) Literature: memory components are kept minority and separately fitted — λ=0.25 post-hoc interpolation [9]; gate trained on held-out, not train CE [17]; n-gram model trained separately and injected as features [18]; memorization-in-parameters demonstrably degrades the shared representations [9]. No identical published setup, so this is anchored extrapolation, not citation of a same experiment. Prescription, cheapest first: (1) log the controller's arm weights and the core-only CE (Engram disabled) — decide from measurements, not priors; (2) cap or delay the Engram's blend weight (fixed small λ or held-out-tuned gate, à la [9,17]); (3) if the core trains fine alone, keep the Engram but judge everything by eval BPB only — train CE is meaningless in this architecture (matches our own 2026-09-26 memory note).

**V3 — lr 1e-4 is wrong for Muon+ at this scale: too low. (Hypothesis (iii): survives, direction now specified.)**
(a) Our numbers: peak 1e-4, batch 5,120 bytes. (b) Moonlight reuses AdamW-optimal lr via update-RMS matching: 8.3–9.5e-4 at 0.4–1.5B params, 4.2e-4 at 16B [11]; Keller's convention: 0.02 [16]. A 7.5M model should sit ≥4.2e-4, plausibly ~1e-3 (RMS-matched convention). A starved core under a self-memorizing memory reproduces both our symptoms at once — core stuck near init (held-out ≈ uniform) while the host-Adam Engram races (train CE → 0.1). Prescription: print per-group update RMS, compare against the AdamW groups' 0.2–0.4 [11]; verify Muon+'s ColRow convention before copying either anchor; sweep 3e-4→3e-3 on short horizons.

**V4 — Data repeats: not the binding constraint. (Hypothesis (iv): dies as stated, one nuance kept.)**
(a) Our numbers: 20 GB unique vs ~150 MB compute-optimal budget [1] — ~130× surplus; we have run zero repeats. (b) Repeats only pay in the token-crisis regime; ≤4 epochs are loss-free [13] but the crisis premise doesn't apply to us. "Replay ratio" is an RL/UTD term, absent from Moonlight (grep-verified) [11,12]. Kept nuance: an early warmup subset re-read ≤4× is permitted by [13] and mimics how every published sub-10M success actually trains (74 epochs of Shakespeare [4]; epochs over TinyStories [5]) — a legitimate accelerator for the core, not a cure for held-out ≈ uniform.

**Priority order implied by the evidence: V3 (lr/update-RMS check, hours) → V2 (measure, then gate the Engram, days) → V1 (re-judge at 150 MB) → V4 (optional warmup repeats).** The one measured fact that outranks everything: held-out is currently *below* a unigram baseline [6], so the core's path to unseen data is broken independently of the data budget.
