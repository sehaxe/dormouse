## 1. The base mechanism (2409.19606), and whether the crate implements it

The brief asked for this explicitly. Short answer: **no, and the deltas are the
point** — `dormouse-mhc` is a transcription of DeepSeek's *constrained* member of the
family (2512.24880), and the constraint plus two layout choices are what make it
a different operator from the base paper's. The Birkhoff projection is **not our
invention** and is not "our own addition on top" — it is a third-party published
mechanism with its own paper, which the crate's header already cites correctly.
What is ours is the *wiring decision* (§3) and the static-bias init (§2.1).

### 1.1 What 2409.19606 actually specifies

Its §2 is short and the mechanism is exact, so there is no reading latitude:

- §2.1: `H⁰ = (h⁰ … h⁰)ᵀ ∈ R^{n×d}` — **the input is `d`-dim and is REPLICATED
  `n` times** to form the hyper-hidden matrix. `n` is the "expansion rate".
- Eq. 1: `HC = [[0, B], [A_m, A_r]] ∈ R^{(n+1)×(n+1)}`, with `B ∈ R^{1×n}`,
  `A_m ∈ R^{n×1}`, `A_r ∈ R^{n×n}`.
- Eq. 2/3/4/5, the whole operator:
  `h₀ᵀ = A_mᵀ H` (**the n streams are mixed DOWN to one `d`-vector — this is the
  layer's input**), `H' = A_rᵀ H` (**the width-connections, `A_r` mixes the n
  streams**), `Ĥ = Bᵀ(T(h₀))ᵀ + H'`.
- Eq. 6/7: the **depth-connections** are `DC = [B; diag(A_r)]` — the residual
  equivalent — and the **width-connections** are `WC = [A_m A_r]`.
- §2.2, DHC: `H̄ = norm(H)`, then `B(H) = s_β ∘ tanh(H̄W_β)ᵀ + B`,
  `A_m(H) = s_α ∘ tanh(H̄W_m) + A_m`, `A_r(H) = s_α ∘ tanh(H̄W_r) + A_r`.
- §2.3 Eq. 14 init: `B^k = 1_{1×n}`, `A_m^k = e_{k mod n}`, `A_r^k = I`.
- **The final readout SUMS the n streams** ("we sum the last hyper hidden matrix
  row-wise to obtain the required hidden vector").
- **So: the layer body `T` runs at width `d`, the residual stream is `n·d`
  wide, and `A_m` is the term that collapses the stream back to `d` before the
  body.** `A_r` is what mixes streams, unconstrained, in `[−∞, ∞]`.

### 1.2 The deltas, one per row

| | **2409.19606 (HC/DHC)** | **`dormouse-mhc` (mHC, 2512.24880)** | file |
|---|---|---|---|
| streams | `n` **replicated copies** of one `d`-vector, total width `n·d` | `n` **disjoint slices** of one `D`-vector, total width `D` | `block.rs:180,190` |
| body width | `d`, fed `A_mᵀ H` | `D`, fed the whole hidden (the caller's body is built at `D`) | §2.2 below |
| readout | **sum** the `n` streams back to `d` | **concatenate** (disjoint slices reassemble to `D`) | `block.rs:199` |
| width-connections | `A_r` free, `A_m` mixes down | `H_res = Sinkhorn(H̃_res)`, **doubly stochastic**; `H_pre` = the `A_m` role | Eq. 8-9 |
| depth-connections | `B` free, no factor | `H_post = 2σ(H̃_post)`, **non-negative, bounded in [0,2]** | Eq. 8 |
| dynamic part | `s ∘ tanh(H̄ W) + static` | `α (H̄ φ) + static`, **no `tanh`** | §2.1 row 3 |
| init | `A_m^k = e_{k mod n}` (a per-layer **rotation**) | `b_pre = b_res = 10`, `b_post = 0` (uniform) | §2.1 last row |
| per-iteration matrices | one per layer (or per sublayer) | **one shared block** at the loop boundary | §3.2 |

Four of these are worth a sentence each, because three of them cut in a direction
a reader might assume goes the other way.

**Slices, not replication — and it is forced, not chosen for expressiveness.**
§3 of mHC builds `x_l` by repeating the `C`-dim state `n` times, so `H_res` mixes
`n` copies of one vector: a pure re-weighting, and `A_m` has to mix the copies
back down to `C` before the body. Slicing a `D`-dim hidden into `n` disjoint
`D/n` chunks gives the same shapes with a different function — ours mixes `n`
quarters of a real hidden state, a genuine cross-stream exchange. The reason it
is *forced* here: our body is built at width `D` and a quarter-width body is a
different model (that is exactly why `H_pre` is not wired, §3.3). Slicing is the
only variant that composes with a full-width body, and the concat readout keeps
`D` so nothing has to be re-learned at the far end.

**Concat, not sum — the layout choice that makes the two consistent.** If we had
replicated, the base paper's "sum the streams" readout would multiply the
readout's input by `n`, so the final projector would have to be retuned (their
§4 does exactly that: scale the output-module stds by `√n`). Slicing makes the
readout width-invariant, so our `out_proj` is untouched — which is one more thing
the arms share, and one fewer confound.

**No `tanh` is now supported by the BASE paper's own ablation, not just by the
printed equations.** §2.1's row 3 argued this from mHC's Eq. 7 dropping it. The
stronger evidence is 2409.19606 Tab. 1, where removing `tanh` is the *better*
variant at every rung on OLMo-1B: V2 eval loss **2.792 vs 2.802** at `n = 2` and
**2.779 vs 2.781** at `n = 4` (and Fig. 5's right subfigure ablates it
separately). So the crate's unbounded linear `φ_*` is a transcription the source
paper's own measurements endorse, and the log-domain Sinkhorn it forces is a
consequence worth paying rather than a bug.

**The base paper's single most important ablation is the axis we changed most.**
Tab. 3, on `DHC×4`: freezing the **width-connections `WC = [A_m A_r]`** costs
**+0.021 V2 / +0.017 V3** loss, while freezing the depth-connections `B` costs
+0.002. We *replace* `A_r` with the Birkhoff projection — a hard constraint on
exactly the matrix the base paper's largest ablation is about — and we do not
wire `A_m` at all. **Row 7b therefore does not measure the base paper's effect;
it measures a family member with one of its two load-bearing blocks changed and
the other dropped.** That is a legitimate experiment, and it is the one the φ
evidence motivates, but it is not "Hyper-Connections" and the A/B row now says so.

### 1.3 The base paper's own expansion-rate ablation is why `n = 2` is first

2409.19606 Tab. 1, OLMo-1B, 500 B tokens, V2 eval loss (lower is better):

| `n` | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | baseline (no HC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DHC | 2.819 | 2.802 | **2.781** | 2.778 | 2.811 |

Three things follow, and all three point the same way:

1. **`n = 1` is WORSE than the residual baseline** (2.819 vs 2.811). Their App. F
   says why: the seesaw effect persists at `n = 1`. This is also why this crate's
   `MhcBlock::new(0, …)` no longer silently trains the `n = 1` arm — a request
   for zero streams is now a loud refusal, because the `n = 1` rung is the one
   the family is *known* to lose at.
2. **Most of the gain arrives by `n = 2`** (2.811 → 2.802, 0.009 of the 0.030
   total) and **`n = 4 → 8` buys 0.003** for double the stream width. The
   paper's own words: *"the increase to n = 8 provides minimal additional
   benefits."* That is the base paper's version of 2607.14530's collapse past
   `N = 4`, and it is why the external review's "n = 2 first" is not a
   compromise between the papers and the box — it is what the base paper's own
   table supports.
3. **We are at `T = 2-4` recurrences, not 16-20 layers.** Their `n` buys width
   the depth-connection matrix can route around; ours has 2-4 slots to route
   around, so the argument for a wide stream is weaker here, not stronger.

### 1.4 The manifold constraint, in two sentences

mHC takes Hyper-Connections' `n`-stream residual stream and projects the
per-token stream-mixing matrix `H_res` onto the **Birkhoff polytope** — the set
of doubly stochastic `n × n` matrices — with 20 iterations of Sinkhorn-Knopp
(Eq. 9). That makes `H_res x_l` a *convex combination* of the streams, so the
forward signal's mean is conserved and `‖H_res‖₂ ≤ 1` bounds the backward gain;
because the set is closed under multiplication, the composite `∏ᵢ H_res^i` over
depth is doubly stochastic too, which is the identity-mapping property plain HC
destroys (HC's own composite reaches Amax gain ≈ 3000 on their 27B, mHC's ≈ 1.6).

`H_pre = σ(·)` and `H_post = 2σ(·)` (Eq. 8) are the *non-negativity* half of the
constraint — the paper calls it "a special manifold projection" — and they are
what stop a branch from being *cancelled* by a negative coefficient. mHC's
measured evidence for the constraint is stability at 27B (Fig. 2's loss surge
and gradient-norm spike for HC at ~12k steps), not a quality win over HC: the
paper's own Tab. 4 has mHC ahead of HC on 7 of 8 benchmarks and behind on MATH
(26.0 vs 26.4).

### 2.2 Three structural differences from the paper, named

(§1.2 has the full delta table against the **base** paper 2409.19606; this is the
crate-internal view of the two that change the operator's *shape*.)
- **Replicated streams → disjoint slices.** §3 of the paper builds `x_l` by
  *repeating* the `C`-dim state `n` times: `x_l = (x_{l,0}ᵀ … x_{l,n-1}ᵀ)ᵀ`. The
  crate instead **slices** the `D`-dim hidden into `n` disjoint chunks of
  `D/n` (`block.rs:180`, `:190`). Same tensor shapes, different function: the
  paper's `H_res` mixes `n` copies of one vector (a pure re-weighting), ours
  mixes `n` quarters of a real hidden state (a genuine cross-stream
  exchange). Ours is the more expressive operator and is the only one that can
  compose with a body that runs at full width `D`. It also means the "identity
  at init" claim is about the operator, not about the stream layout.
- **The body is not per-stream.** In the paper `F` is one function mapping
  `C → C`. Ours is one function mapping `D → D`, so the crate reads its output
  as `n` per-stream outputs and weights each by `H_post_j`. The `[1,n]`-shaped
  `H_post` broadcasting over `[b,t,n,C]` is what makes that work.
- **Sum → concat at the readout.** The base paper (2409.19606 §2.1) collapses
  the `n` streams back to one `d`-vector by **summing** them, and compensates in
  §4 by scaling every output module's std by `√n`. We **concatenate**: the
  disjoint slices reassemble to exactly `D`, so `out_proj` is untouched and
  needs no rescaling. This is a consequence of slicing rather than replicating,
  not an independent choice, and it is one fewer difference between the two arms
  in row 7b.

## 3. PLACEMENT, decided explicitly

### 3.1 Where in the chain

```
ReZero (default)  |  use_gr -> Gated Residual  |  use_attnres -> AttnRes  |  use_mhc -> mHC
```

All four replace **one statement** — `loop_block.rs`'s residual write,
`h = h_ctx + y·scale` — so any two are refused together in `config::validate`,
now by a single three-arm check (`validation.rs:85-115`) so a future fourth arm
cannot leave a pair unguarded. The branch is `loop_block.rs:571-604`; the
`forward_full_state` assert at `:319-324` re-checks it for a hand-built block
that bypassed the config path.

### 3.2 Per-iteration connections, ONE shared block — and why

`LoopBlock::mhc: Option<MhcBlock>` is a **single** block applied at every
iteration's write, not `Vec<MhcBlock>` of length `max_iter`. Three reasons, in
descending order of how much they would cost if I got it wrong:

1. **The manifold's argument is about the COMPOSITE.** 2512.24880 Eq. 4 and
   §4.1 are entirely about `∏ᵢ H_res^i` across depth. A shared block makes the
   loop's composite `H_res^T` — a power of a doubly stochastic matrix, still in
   the Birkhoff polytope, because that set is closed under multiplication. So
   the constraint's central claim survives our depth `T = 2-4` *exactly*, and
   `mhc_res_leaves_the_identity_when_the_bias_moves_and_stays_on_the_manifold`
   asserts the closure on the real `4×4` composite.
2. **Weight sharing is the loop's defining property.** Per-slot residual
   matrices would make the residual operator the only un-shared thing in a
   block that shares its controller, attention, memory, experts, norm and
   readout. `iter_embed` and `residual_scale` are shared for the same reason.
3. **The connection count has to survive `T` small — and `n` is cheap in
   parameters and *irrelevant* to launches, which is the whole latency story.**
   `mhc_streams` **defaults to 2**, changed from 4 on the external review of
   2026-10-01 (*"начать с n=2, одной вариацией за запуск"*; and at 9.2M
   2605.09165/2510.26692 answered *"недостаточно свидетельств"*), because `K = 2`
   is exactly what the φ study ran (§0) and exactly what 2409.19606 Tab. 1
   supports (§1.3). The crate's `n` is a constructor argument —
   `MhcBlock::new(n_branches, d_model, device)` — so this was a one-flag change
   and not a fork; `--set mhc_streams=4` is the follow-up row.
   The three papers that put the knee at 4 are still the right reason not to
   climb:
   - **the base paper measures the ladder** (2409.19606v3 §4.1, Tab. 1, OLMo-1B,
     500 B tokens, V2 eval loss): baseline **2.811**, `DHC×1` **2.819** (worse
     than the baseline — `n = 1` is the one rung the family loses at), `DHC×2`
     2.802, `DHC×4` **2.781**, `DHC×8` 2.778. So `n = 4` buys 0.021 and `n = 8`
     buys 0.003 more.
   - **mHC itself ships `n = 4`** (2512.24880 App. A.1 Table 5) — which is why
     this is a deliberate departure from mHC's own default and not an oversight.
   - **2607.14530 (xHC)** finds the family's gains collapse past `N = 4`, with
     the two causes named: "insufficient write-back information for an expanding
     number of streams" and residual-mixing generation that "cost scales
     cubically with N".

   At `n = 2` the block costs `2·D·n + D·n² + 2n + n² + 3` = **6 155**
   parameters on `small` — measured on the instantiated block at
   `d_model = 768`, 0.067 % of `small`'s 9 197 390, and 0.067 % of nothing that
   matters. (An earlier draft of this file said `3·D·n + D·n² + n² + 5n` = 2 306
   "at `D = 384`"; the formula was wrong by the `phi_pre` term and the width was
   wrong because **`small` is `d_model = 768`, not 384** — §3.2's own numbers,
   caught by the same gate that now asserts the count against the built block.)
   So parameters were never the binding constraint at any `n`. What binds is the
   mixing, and `n = 1` is the published
   proof that the mixing is the whole point: at `n = 1` this arm is a *worse*
   ReZero with a Sinkhorn attached. That is also why `MhcBlock::new` refuses
   `n = 0` loudly rather than clamping it to 1 (see §4.9), and why
   `config::validate` refuses `mhc_streams = 0` for the same reason.

   **The cost note is NOT an argument for a larger `n`, and I got this wrong
   before the review.** I first wrote in `schema.rs` that "`n` is a
   launch-count knob". It is not. `sinkhorn_knopp` issues a fixed
   `SINKHORN_ITERS × 2 directions × 7 ops = 280` forward launches per call
   (`sinkhorn.rs:48-70`), plus a 40-step hand-derived reverse unroll in the
   backward, and **that count does not depend on `n` at all** — `n` scales the
   element count of each tiny `[b, t, n, n]` tensor and the parameter count,
   never the number of kernels the host enqueues. So **a ladder in `n` will not
   buy the latency back**; only the fused kernel would, and it is deliberately
   off (§6). The consequence for the experiment is in §6 and in row 7b: step
   time is priced as a *separate* verdict from BPB, and a smaller `n` is not
   the lever that would make it go away.

**The loop boundary, not between sublayers.** This is the paper's placement, not
mine: 2604.21106v3 §5.2 puts the lanes *across loops*, and cites 2604.21254
("loop-level hyperconnections beat layer-level"). Our iterations are the loops.

### 3.3 `H_pre` is not wired, and the cost is 11 %

Eq. 3 is `x' = H_res x + H_postᵀ F(H_pre x, W)`, and `H_pre x` is
`[1,n]·[n,C] = [1,C]` — it feeds the block's **own** function `F`, collapsing
the `n × C` stream to `C`. Our body is built at width `D`; running it on a
`C = D/4`-dimensional input means building a second, quarter-width block, which
is a different model and a different experiment. The paper's own Tab. 1 says
what that costs:

| arms enabled | absolute loss gap |
|---|---|
| none | 0.0 |
| `H_res` only | **−0.022** |
| `H_res` + `H_pre` | −0.025 |
| all three | −0.027 |

`H_res` is 81 % of the total; `H_pre` adds 11 % and `H_post` 7 %. We wire
`H_res` + `H_post` = **89 % of the measured effect** and leave the 11 % to a
decision that needs a quarter-width block.

The cost of that choice is concrete and asserted rather than absorbed:
`phi_pre` (`[D, n]`) plus its gate `alpha_pre` and static bias `b_pre` are
**allocated and never read** — 1 540 + 1 + 2 = **1 543 parameters** on `small`
at `n = 2`, 0.017 % of the model, and they are not in the graph at all, so
`grad()` returns `None` for them rather than zeros.
`mhc_gradients_reach_every_wired_parameter` asserts exactly that for all three,
with a message that fires if something ever starts using `H_pre`.

### 3.4 The base state is `h_ctx`, not `h`

ReZero writes `h = h_ctx + y·s` where `h_ctx = h + e_k`. The mHC write uses
`h_ctx` as `x_l` for the same reason: **the A/B against ReZero must differ in the
residual operator and in nothing else.** Using `h` would move the iteration
embedding from the write to the read side and confound the one comparison the
arm exists to make. `mhc_and_rezero_each_move_only_their_own_write` asserts both
directions of that separation, including that `residual_scale` is inert under
mHC.

## 4. Defect classes hunted in dormouse-mhc, proactively

The two classes the paper-faithful AttnRes wiring found an hour before this
(`511daa5`, AGENTS §2.2): *a derivative factor that vanishes under a norm* and
*an absolute-tolerance gate on a value that moved scale*. Plus the
`dormouse-rmsnorm` class: *a test target that compiles to nothing.*

### 4.1 FOUND — a SILENT 1e-7 floor in the fused kernel, untested, in the same
class as the defect the log-domain rewrite exists to fix

`sinkhorn_cuda.rs:89-97` (row) and `:105-111` (column) clamp the normalization
sum to `1e-7` **in the linear domain**, with no counter and no escape:

```rust
let mut rn = rs;
if rn < F::new(1e-7_f32) { rn = F::new(1e-7_f32); }
```

The tensor path (`sinkhorn.rs:47-70`) runs the same normalization in the **log
domain** and has no floor at all. Its own module doc (`sinkhorn.rs:13-21`) says
why: entries above `exp(88)` overflow f32, and bounded inputs whose within-row
range exceeds f32's exponent span flush to zero, "after which a zero column sum
makes the column pass emit inf and the next pass NaN". So the kernel
**reinstates in linear space the exact failure the tensor path was rewritten to
remove**: a row whose true sum is `1e-20` is divided by `1e-7` and comes out
`1e13` times too large, where the tensor path returns the `1e-20` and converges.

- **Not reachable from `MhcBlock` at init** (`b_res = 10I` puts the diagonal at
  `e^10`), so it is not a training hazard at step 0.
- **No test covers it.** `sinkhorn_large_logits_stay_finite` (`lib.rs:93-125`)
  exercises the *non-autodiff* path only — the inventory doc already named the
  autodiff fallback's untested overflow; this is the *kernel* floor and it is a
  third instance.
- **ADR-0011 mark: it carries SILENT, and SILENT is a defect.** The fix is
  COUNTED (a counter next to `FWD`/`BWD`) or the row is dropped in favour of the
  log-domain formulation the tensor path already has.
- **Not fixed in this commit**: the kernel cannot be run or measured on this
  lane's card, and a fix I cannot test is a fix I cannot claim. Named at the
  line (`sinkhorn_cuda.rs:89-99`) so the next reader meets it there, and it is
  the first precondition of the CUDA follow-up in §7.

### 4.2 FOUND — an absolute tolerance on a quantity whose scale is a free
parameter

`ad_tests::sinkhorn_fused_backward_matches_tensor`

a relative requirement rather than guessing an absolute one. Not changed here:

Also in this class, smaller: `n1_identity_at_init` (`lib.rs:128-139`) asserts

### 4.5 CHECKED, CLEAN — the derivative factors under the norm

The AttnRes defect was a hand-written adjoint missing a factor that vanished at
the L2 norm, so every test stayed green. In this crate:

- **The RMSNorm's derivative is burn's**, not ours. `x_norm`
  (`block.rs:76-85`) is plain tensor arithmetic, so `∂(h/‖h‖)/∂h` is
  autocomputed. There is no hand-written factor to drop, and the `1e-20` is
  the paper's ε, not a fudge that could hide one.
- **The hand-written adjoint IS the Sinkhorn's**, and it is correct. For
  `m_post = m_pre / s`, `s = Σⱼ m_preⱼ`:
  `∂m_postᵢ/∂m_preⱼ = δᵢⱼ/s − m_preᵢ/s²`, so
  `∂L/∂m_preⱼ = dⱼ/s − (Σᵢ dᵢ m_preᵢ)/s²`. Both
  `sinkhorn_backward_tensor` (`sinkhorn_cuda.rs:480-506`) and the fused
  `sinkhorn_backward_kernel` (`:168-196`) compute exactly that, recover
  `m_pre = m_post·s` rather than storing it, and chain to the logits with the
  `· exp(logits)` Jacobian of `M⁽⁰⁾ = exp(H̃_res)`. I re-derived all three
  pieces and found no missing factor, no sign error and no wrong axis (the
  tensor version's `if j % 2 == 0 { 3 } else { 2 }` correctly alternates row/
  column with the unroll index, and it is unrolled in reverse).
- **No norm in the Sinkhorn path**, so the "vanishes under a norm" shape has
  nowhere to hide.

### 5.1 `(a)` FORM FIDELITY — three gates, because "the readout moved" is the
gate that let a `d^-0.5` scale through

**`eq3_residual_is_the_papers_equation_on_a_hand_computed_fixture`**
(`dormouse-mhc/src/lib.rs`). `n = 2`, `C = 2`, `D = 4`, so the whole residual is
four numbers a human can check. `α_* = 0` kills the dynamic part, leaving
`H_post = 2σ(b_post)` and `H_res = SK(b_res)` exactly.

`b_res = [[0, −ln4], [−ln4, 0]]` → `M = exp(b_res) = [[1, ¼], [¼, 1]]`. Sinkhorn's
alternating normalization preserves the cross ratio `M₀₀M₁₁/(M₀₁M₁₀) = 16`, and
a 2×2 doubly stochastic `[[p,1−p],[q,1−q]]` has cross ratio `p(1−q)/((1−p)q)`,
which is 16 exactly at `p = q = 4/5`. **20 iterations reach `[[0.8,0.2],[0.2,0.8]]`
to the last bit of f32** (verified numerically before writing the test, so the
expected value is the exact literal 0.8, not a tolerance band). With
`b_post = [−0.5, 1.5]` → `H_post = [0.75508134, 1.63514895]`, Eq. 3 gives, for
stream `j` and column `c`, `out[j,c] = Σᵢ H_res[j,i]·x[i,c] + H_post[j]·y[j,c]`
with `x = [[1,2],[3,4]]` and `y = [[10,20],[30,40]]`. **All four numbers**, in the
row-major order `reshape([b,t,n,C])` returns:

| | `c = 0` | `c = 1` |
|---|---|---|
| `j = 0` | `0.8·1 + 0.2·3 + 0.75508134·10` = **8.95081338** | `0.8·2 + 0.2·4 + 0.75508134·20` = 17.50162675 |
| `j = 1` | `0.2·1 + 0.8·3 + 1.63514895·30` = 51.65446857 | `0.2·2 + 0.8·4 + 1.63514895·40` = 69.00595810 |

**The generalisable rule, in the AttnRes/`511daa5` family:** *a gate whose
expected values are literals is a gate that must have been observed to go red.*
A red→green table row is a claim about a run. If the run is not in evidence,
the row is a story — and this one was in a file whose whole subject is the
cost of stories that look like measurements.

**`mhc_res_is_doubly_stochastic_in_both_directions`** (model level). The
operator's defining property, in both directions, as the paper's own Amax Gain
Magnitude metric: forward `max|rowsum − 1|` and backward `max|colsum − 1|`.
Different bounds on purpose — the last normalization of Eq. 9 is a row pass, so
the row sums are exact to fp and the column sums are the unconverged direction,
which is what the paper's Fig. 7(a) measures. Non-negativity (Eq. 6) too. This
gate cannot be written for ReZero (a scalar has no matrix) or AttnRes (a softmax
over sources has no stream mixing), which is the point: it tests the operator
CLASS, not the output.

**`mhc_res_leaves_the_identity_when_the_bias_moves_and_stays_on_the_manifold`**
(the one that matters). Two ends of the trajectory, because the middle is where
it fails:
- at the paper's init `b_res = 10I`, `H_res` **is** the identity to `4.5e-5` —
  and that is the *point* of the constraint (§4.1, "restores the identity mapping
  property"), not a defect, so it is asserted, against the **predicted**
  `(n−1)·e^{−10} = 4.54e-5` rather than a tolerance band;
- perturbed off the init, `H_res` must **move** (asserted `> 0.5` from the
  identity, measured 0.8809) **and stay on the manifold** (row `< 1e-3`, col
  `< 5e-2`), and the `T = 4` **composite** must still be doubly stochastic *and
  converge toward uniform mixing*.

That middle assertion is aimed at a **published failure mode of this exact
constraint**: 2603.20896 (s²HC, NeurIPS 2026) reports that under double
stochasticity "learned matrices collapse around the identity initialization and
diminish cross-stream interactions". If our `b_res` perturbation left the
composite at the identity, the arm would be a slower ReZero and row 7b would be
measuring nothing. `2602.18308` (JPmHC) attacks the same manifold from the
Jacobian-spectrum side, and `2607.14530` (xHC) reports the family's gains
collapsing past `N = 4`. **Three papers attacking the mechanism we are wiring
in, all found by reading around the citation rather than inside it** — the same
move that found AttnRes's `d^-0.5`.

#### 5.1b MOVING `n` FROM 4 TO 2 BROKE THE COMPOSITE ASSERTION, AND THE GATE WAS
#### WRONG

The composite half of the gate used to assert that the off-diagonal mass of
`H_res^T` **grows toward the fixed point** `n−1` by a factor of 5, stated as
"the paper's §4.1 point 3: repeated application *tends to increase the mixing of
information across streams monotonically*". That reads as a fair transcription.
**It is false at `n = 2`, and it was only ever true at `n = 4` by luck.**

A symmetric doubly stochastic `H_res` has a second eigenvalue that is
**negative** — measured `{1, −0.7616}` at `n = 2` and `{1, −0.2758 ×3}` at
`n = 4` — so `H_res^T` **alternates** about the fixed point instead of
approaching it from one side. Off-diagonal mass at `n = 2`, computed from Eq. 9
before the gate was edited:

| `T` | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off-diagonal mass | 1.7616 | **0.4200** | 1.4417 | 0.6636 | 1.2562 | 0.8049 | 0.8868 |
| distance to `J/n = 1` | 0.7616 | 0.5800 | 0.4417 | 0.3364 | 0.2562 | 0.1951 | 0.1132 |

The off-diagonal mass **overshoots past the uniform matrix and comes back** — at
`T = 2` a two-stream residual is *less* mixed than a single pass. The old
assertion wanted `|off(T) − (n−1)| < |off(1) − (n−1)|/5`, i.e. 0.3364 < 0.1523:
**it would have failed on a correct implementation.** At `n = 4` the same
fixture happened to land 0.0174 from the fixed point at `T = 4`, so the gate was
green for a reason that had nothing to do with the property.

What *does* hold at every `T` and both `n` is the **distance to `J/n`**, because
`|λ| < 1` makes `|λ|^T` monotone. So the gate now asserts that, and the numbers
are printed and match the prediction to four decimals: `0.7615 → 0.3363`
(predicted `0.7616 → 0.3364`).

**Why this matters beyond the fixture, and it is a prediction about our
operator class, not a test quibble:** at `T = 2-4` a two-stream mHC residual
*oscillates* around uniform stream mixing rather than settling into it. The
manifold still holds at every step — that part is exact, the Birkhoff polytope is
closed under multiplication, and the gate asserts it — but "the loop converges
toward uniform mixing" is a claim about the *envelope*, and at `n = 2, T = 2-4`
the envelope is the one the distance-to-`J/n` series describes. Anyone reading
`b_res` off a run should read the composite, not the single matrix.

### 5.3 `(c)` ZERO-DEFAULT IDENTITY — and the honest limit of what one process
can witness

Two gates, because "bitwise unchanged" is not available in-process:

1. **`mhc_and_rezero_each_move_only_their_own_write`.** ReZero's knob
   (`residual_scale`: 1.0 → 0.5) moves the ReZero arm and **cannot be
   expressed** on the mHC arm; `b_res` moves the mHC arm; and `residual_scale`
   on an mHC model is asserted to change the output by **exactly 0.0**. A
   branch-ordering slip — the new `else if` swallowing ReZero's statement, or
   ReZero's scalar leaking into the mHC write — is invisible to "did the output
   change" and fatal here. Plus `mhc.is_none()` when the flag is off, which is
   the load-bearing checkpoint fact: an `Option<MhcBlock>` that is `None`
   contributes **no records** to burnpack, so every shipped preset's state dict
   is unchanged.
2. **`mhc_at_init_is_rezero_at_scale_one`.** mHC's init computes `h + y`, which
   is ReZero's write at its own init (`residual_scale = 1`, asserted `== 1.0`).
   Measured relative difference: **asserted `< 1e-2` and `> 1e-6`** — the lower
   bound so an arm that is *bitwise* ReZero (i.e. the projection is not in the
   write at all) fails. This is also the A/B's confound, quantified: the two arms
   start from the same function to within that measured relative error, and differ
   in the operator. Without it, the lower bound would say the arm is inert and
   the upper bound would say the arms start from different functions; both are
   real failure modes and both are checked.

**What is NOT claimed, stated so nobody reads more into it:** a bitwise-equal CE
against a build from before this flag existed is a **cross-process** fact (two
RNG streams, two process-global `ParamId` counters) and belongs to
`tools/determinism.py` / `tools/determinism/`, not to a unit test. The
diff-level fact is that the ReZero branch's code is unmodified — the new arm is
an `else if` ahead of it.

### 5.3a TWO GATES OF MY OWN THAT WERE VACUOUS, CAUGHT BY RUNNING THEM

Both of these passed on the first run and would have shipped as evidence. They
are the AttnRes lesson (`511daa5`: "a gate that cannot fail and a gate that fails
for a reason unrelated to what it names are the same defect from two
directions") aimed at me, and both are the same root cause: **in-process RNG.**

1. **`mhc_at_init_is_rezero_at_scale_one` compared two different networks.** The
   obvious spelling builds a ReZero `LoopBlock` and an mHC one and compares their
   outputs. `LoopBlock::new` draws every weight from the device RNG, and two
   constructions in one process get two different weight sets — `Device::seed`
   does not rewind a consumed stream, and `Device::flex()` hands back a *shared*
   device (the 2026-09-30 finding). Measured: **relative difference 1.686**, i.e.
   the "arms differ only in the residual operator" claim was being tested
   against two random initialisations. Fixed by toggling `use_mhc` on **one**
   block: same weights, same body, same readout, one statement apart. The red
   reading is the number that makes this worth recording.
2. **`mhc_differs_from_both_rezero_and_attnres_at_every_depth` was the same
   mistake wearing three arms** — three `LoopBlock::new` calls, so three weight
   sets, so three different outputs *whatever the residual operator was*. It
   would have passed with three copies of the same arm. Replaced by
   `the_three_residual_operators_are_three_functions`, which compares the three
   residual **writes** on one `h` and one `y` (`MhcBlock::forward` vs `h + y·s`
   vs `depth_attend` over the same two states), with no model construction in it
   at all, and keeps the model-level half as a separate, honestly-weak
   `mhc_moves_the_readout_at_every_depth` (also one block, toggled).

**The generalisable rule, added here because it cost two gates:** *any
comparison between two `Module`s built in the same process is a comparison
between two RNG draws unless the weights are shared or transferred.* The
existing gates in this file all do the second thing. `model_seam`'s
`two_models_one_seed_are_bit_identical` is the test that was withdrawn for
exactly this reason on 2026-09-30; I walked into the same floor two hours later
and only a printed number caught it.

### 5.3b A THIRD, WHICH WAS A WRONG PREDICTION RATHER THAN A WRONG GATE

`mhc_res_leaves_the_identity…` perturbs `b_res` and asserts the mixing operator
leaves the identity. My first perturbation added ±1.75/2.5 to the init's
`b_res = 10 I`, which moves the **diagonal** to 12.5 and leaves the
off-diagonals near zero — a *more* extreme identity, not a less one. It read
`1.9e-6` and the message blamed identity degeneration. The gate was right and
the perturbation was wrong.

What that accident measures, and it is a property of the ARM rather than of the
test: **near the paper's init the map is exponentially stiff.** One unit on a
single off-diagonal of `b_res` moves `H_res` by **2.1e-4**, because the diagonal
sits at `e^10` and the off-diagonal ratio is `e^-10`. So an `H_res` that training
has not yet moved is *exactly* the identity, and the arm's whole contribution
in its first steps is `H_post ≈ 1` — i.e. a ReZero. The measured gradients agree:
`b_res` 1.8e-6, `alpha_res` **1.0e-7**. The dynamic half of the hyper-network
(`alpha_res · x̃'φ_res`, Eq. 7) is damped twice over — by the 1e-2 gate *and* by a
Sinkhorn sitting on its identity. **A run whose `b_res` and `alpha_res` have not
left init has not started the mechanism**, and row 7b says so. §7 owes a cheap
log line for it, because the two numbers that decide this arm are not the loss
curve's.

## 6. Cost, and what the fused path is worth here

**A machine fact, recorded because it is new and it is a precondition.**
`/home` reached **99 % (7.1 GB free)** during this task, and the cause is
visible in one command: `du -sh /home/sehaxe/dormouse-wt/*/target` — eight lanes
each carrying a private target dir, 3.8 GB to 11 GB apiece, on top of the
main repo's 113 GB. The build lock serialises *cargos*; it does not serialise
*disk*. AGENTS.md §2.5 already says "a mold error that says 'Disk full?' is the
disk", and that is now a near-term risk rather than a historical one. This lane
contributed almost nothing (it used the shared main target dir precisely because
a cold worktree build does not fit in 7 GB) but its worktree picked up a 3.1 GB
`vendor/dormouse-fused/target` from the concurrent writer of §4.9. **Recommendation
to the owner, not a change made here:** one shared `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` plus
per-lane incremental dirs, or a `target/` sweep on lane exit. The worktree script
cleans nothing.

`SINKHORN_ITERS = 20` on an `n × n` matrix is **not** a GEMM: per call the
tensor path issues 20 iterations × (2 `max_dim` + 2 `sub` + `exp` + `sum_dim` +
`log` + `add` + `sub`) × 2 phases = **280 tiny launches** on a `b·t`-batched
`[n,n]` tensor, plus a 40-step hand-derived reverse unroll in the backward, times
`T` iterations per forward. On a workload whose GPU is idle 87 % of a warm step
(§3.1) that is a launch-bound cost, not a bandwidth one, and it is the arm's
dominant expense.

#### 6.1 The launch count does NOT depend on `n`, and the A/B prices step time
#### as a separate verdict because of it

This is the sharpest correction on the 2026-10-01 pass, and it inverts the
obvious plan. The natural hope is that a smaller `n` is also a cheaper `n` — and
it is not, for the launch-bound reason that motivates the whole concern:

| | depends on `n`? | at `n = 2` vs `n = 4` |
|---|---|---|
| parameter count | **yes** | **6 155** vs **18 459** on `small` (`d_model = 768`) — 0.067 % vs 0.201 % of `small`, irrelevant either way. `2Dn + Dn² + 2n + n² + 3`, **asserted against the instantiated block** by `mhc_counts_every_iteration_and_is_refused_alongside_the_others` rather than asserted on paper: an earlier version of this table carried 2 306 / 9 253, which was wrong twice over (it used `d_model = 384`, and `small` is 768). |
| elements per tiny tensor | **yes** (`n²` per token) | 4 vs 16 — both far too small to fill an SM |
| **kernel launches per call** | **NO** | **280 forward + 40-step backward unroll, identical** |

So the external review's launch-bound warning — *"at our 13 % GPU utilisation,
MHC's extra kernel launches may cost MORE than its parameter count"* — is
**exactly right, and it cuts further than the review stated**: the launches do
not just exceed the parameter count, they are *invariant* to the one knob the
review told us to turn. `n = 2` is the right first rung because it is the `K`
the φ evidence used and what the base paper's Tab. 1 supports (§1.3), **not**
because it is cheaper at runtime. Anyone expecting the `n = 2` arm to run faster
than an `n = 4` arm will be disappointed, and the only thing that collapses
those 280 launches is the fused kernel.

**Consequence for the experiment, and it is why row 7b has two verdicts.** The
review's condition is *"убедиться что экономия качества компенсирует рост
latency"* — confirm the quality saving offsets the latency growth. That cannot be
answered from a BPB column, so row 7b now requires, as a **separate** reported
number:

- **Δ step ms and Δ %**, from `--timers`, **at a step index ≥ 50**, on a quiet
  card, at the same batch size in both arms, several steps averaged. The
  `step ≥ 50` is not pedantry: AGENTS §2.2/§3.1 record a warm step of ~245 ms
  against 5 549 ms at step 0 — a **23×** spread that is entirely the cubecl
  autotune cache. A step-time reading with no step index is a reading of the
  cache, and this arm's cost is exactly the kind a cold cache hides.
- **and** the fwd/bwd split, because the 280 launches land in the forward and the
  40-step unroll in the backward; if the two halves are comparable the arm is
  cheaper to fix (one fused forward) than the aggregate suggests.

**The verdict rule is stricter than the BPB rule alone:** the arm lands only if
BPB wins by more than the control's 3-seed spread **and** the step-time delta is
affordable at the program's operating depth. The reviewer's own verdict at our
scale — *"пока не внедрять"* — is honoured structurally: `use_mhc` defaults off,
`Option<MhcBlock>::None` contributes no records to burnpack, every shipped preset
loads unchanged, and the cost of a negative verdict is one flag.

**That is the argument for eventually enabling `dormouse-mhc/cuda`** — and the
argument against doing it now. `dormouse-core/Cargo.toml` carries the
dependency with a comment saying so: the fused kernel is reachable on the
trainer's bare `CubeBackend` (§4.7), it would collapse those launches into one,
but it has the untested 1e-7 floor (§4.1), an unmeasured exact backward (§4.2),
and a fallback with **no counter** — ADR-0019's open COUNTED item in this crate.
Swapping the tensor path that every CPU gate above pins, for an unmeasured
kernel, on a card this lane does not own, would be exactly the silent swap the
doctrine exists to prevent. The cost is named, not hidden, and the A/B's cost
column stays `unknown` for this reason as well as §3.3's.

**A same-week data point from the adjacent lane, which is why this decision is
not merely cautious.** `d8fa449` (2026-10-01, `tests/kda_param_grads_cuda.rs`)
answered the `8fa5d4c` question *yes* on the trainer's backend and, in the same
commit, found that **dormouse-gdn2's fused adjoint is wrong and non-deterministic**
(4.5e-2..2.6e-1 from a central difference of its own forward, on q/k/v,
`beta_proj` and `decay.b_alpha`, while three post-op parameters matched an
independent CPU autodiff at 2e-7) and that **a fused forward launches on the
trainer's backend and its result is discarded** — the ADR-0019 silent-fallback
shape, `fused kda=0/0` meaning "not on this path" rather than "slow". So the
fused-kernel family in this repo now has one *proven* case of a hand-derived
adjoint that compiles, engages, and is numerically wrong. `dormouse-mhc`'s adjoint is
the same shape of thing (hand-derived, 40-step reverse unroll, gated only by a
`to_vec`-visible counter) and has **never been compared to anything but its own
tensor path**. That is the case for keeping the tensor path on tonight's build
and for the order in §7: the gradient gate (§4.3) and the floor (§4.1) come
*before* the feature flag, not after.

## 7. Owed, in the order it should be paid

1. **Eval-line field `mhc=<ran>/<asked>`.** `probe::MHC` exists and is gated;
   the print is in the hot `train/src/lib.rs`, which another lane owns, so it is
   not wired here — same debt AttnRes carried, and `mhc` is already in
   `probe::NAMES` so the one-line field is all that is missing.
2. **`fused_node_carries_a_gradient`**, §4.3 —
   `cargo test -p dormouse-mhc --features cuda,autodiff -- --ignored`. The one clause
   that cannot be checked anywhere else. Until it runs, **"the fused Sinkhorn
   trains" is unverified**, exactly as `8fa5d4c`'s own message said about itself.
3. **The 1e-7 floor** (§4.1): a counter or the log-domain formulation, plus a
   test at extreme logits that exercises the KERNEL rather than the tensor path.
4. **`dormouse-mhc/cuda` on `dormouse-core`** (§6), gated on 2 and 3.
5. **The `cfg(cuda)` on a CPU test** (§4.4) and the relative tolerance on
   `sinkhorn_fused_backward_matches_tensor` (§4.2). Whoever has the card.
6. **`H_pre`**, if a quarter-width body is ever wanted (§3.3) — 11 % of the
   paper's measured effect, and a different experiment.
7. **`mhc_streams = 4`**, the base paper's own App. Tab. 1 rung and mHC's shipped
   default — the follow-up row after `n = 2`, and the capacity ladder below
   `n = 4` that 2607.14530 says does not pay. Note before spending anything on
   it: **the launch count is the same at `n = 4`**, so this rung buys quality,
   not latency (§6.1), and row 7b's step-time verdict does not improve.
8. **A non-symmetric `b_res` fixture** for Eq. 3 (§5.1a). The current one is
   symmetric, so `H_res` and `H_resᵀ` are indistinguishable and the gate cannot
   catch a transposed residual matrix — the single most plausible remaining
   transcription error in the operator.
9. **The eval line must also report `b_res`'s distance from its init**, not just
   the `mhc` counter (§5.3b): the arm's first N steps are a ReZero with a
   Sinkhorn in front of it, and a run whose `b_res` and `alpha_res` have not left
   init has not started the mechanism. The two numbers that decide this arm are
   not on the loss curve.
