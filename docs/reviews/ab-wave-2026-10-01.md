sample sd 0.0532, range 0.1290. That is a number about the *union* of two
families and it is the wrong yardstick for a wave that runs **aux ON**.

**The yardstick for this wave is the aux-ON 3-seed control:**

| | n | mean | sd (sample) | range (max−min) | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **control, aux ON** | 3 | **6.3433** | 0.0386 | **0.0730** | 6.314 | 6.387 |

**§1.2's bar is 0.0730 BPB** — the control's own seed range — and a win has to
beat it, not beat its own sd. Both numbers are in the table so a reader can
apply either reading.

### 0.2 The seeds are 1/2/3, and "1001/1002/1003" is a filename

`first_run.sh`'s log pattern is `first_run_${STEPS}_$(date +%m%d_%H%M).log`.
`first_run_2000_1001_0037.log` is **Oct 01, 00:37** — `1001` is the *date* and
`0037` the time, not a seed. The seeds on record are 1, 2, 3 (from the ckpt
names `--seed-2-`, `--seed-3-` and the `seed = 1` in the config snapshot).

So the wave runs **seeds 1/2/3**, paired against the control's seeds 1/2/3. This
is deliberate and it is the better design: `--seed` is a pure function of model
init and the JEPA mask (§3.7), so a same-seed pair starts from the same
initialisation to within the measured TSCT residue (≤1.04e-06 relative), which
removes the init term from the comparison instead of adding a second variance
source. Running seeds 1001/1002/1003 would have compared against a control
measured on 1/2/3 and charged the difference to the arm.

### 0.3 The recipe, read off `checkpoints/first_run_2000--retract-every-4-.config.toml`

`--preset small --batch 8 --seq-len 512 --no-engram --retract-every 4
--steps 2000 --eval-every 500 --guard --detach --timers --seed <1|2|3>`, aux at
the preset defaults (**jepa 0.05 + KoLeo, dspark 0.0**), `eval_batches 20`,
`lr 1e-4 cosine`, `opt mix` (Muon+ ColRow ns=8), `quant` auto → **Fp8** on
sm_120, `max_iter 4`, `rank 64`, `n_experts 3`, `d_model 768`, `d_ffn 2048`,
params 9 197 454. Launched by `tools/first_run.sh` — the control family was
launched by that same script, and the ckpt-name formula reproduces its names
exactly (`--retract-every 4` → `first_run_2000--retract-every-4-`, which is how
the trailing `-` on every one of those names is accounted for: `tr -c` maps the
newline).

**A confound I am naming rather than hiding: the 3-seed control family was run
at `retract_every 1`, and this wave runs `retract_every 4`** (the owner's working
mode, and the mode the 100k is on). The cadence effect is measured at **one
seed only**: seed 1 gives 6.387 (retract 1) vs **6.329** (retract 4), a paired
−0.058. So the arm family is compared against a 3-seed retract-1 control plus a
single retract-4 point. If the wave finishes with time in hand, two retract-4
control seeds (2/3) close this; if it does not, §7 lists it as the open
precision gap.

## 1. The queue as briefed, and the two places I departed from it

Brief order: (1) wait for the 100k + record + board, (2) the two-chunk adjoint
CUDA gate in `wt/kda-adjoint`, (3) generation from the 100k export, (4) the
6-arm × 3-seed wave, (5) per-arm verdict vs the control spread.

Two changes, both forced and both recorded here rather than silently taken:

* **Item 2 runs from `main`, not `wt/kda-adjoint`.** The brief says the lane's
  worktree. But `wt/kda-adjoint` has **no `target/`**, and a cold build of one
  test binary in it is the 686-package / ~41 GB / ~23 min figure the build lock
  was written for — against **29 GB free on `/home` at 94 %**. It does not fit,
  and starting it risks a disk-full mid-link (`§2.5`: "a mold error that says
  'Disk full?' is the disk"). Instead: `diff -rq` of
  `vendor/burn-fused/crates/burn-gdn2` between `main` and `wt/kda-adjoint`
  reports **no differences at all** except two `__pycache__` `.pyc` files. The
  lane's test landed on `main` as `439d8c8` (the same change as its `c3f5c31`),
  and `main` has a **warm 98 GB `target/debug`**. So the identical gate runs
  from `main` for the price of one target's link.
* **Item 2 is placed after item 3.** Item 2 costs a build (~5-15 min) that item
  3 does not, and item 4 is the owner's gate #1 ("all arms A/B'd at 3 seeds")
  and needs ~6.3 h of a window that has ~6.5 h left when the 100k exits.
  Spending 40 min of GPU-serial time on a diagnostic before the deliverable
  would cost one arm. Item 2 therefore runs **immediately after the first arm's
  first seed** (its build overlaps nothing, and it is bounded at ~2 min of CUDA).

Everything else is as briefed: one CUDA process at a time, `pgrep` preflight
before every launch, all builds through `tools/build_lock.sh run abwave --`,
logs in `~/logs/`, sequential arms, no push, no deletions, no config-number
changes.

### 2.1 RoPE — the arm is not reachable from `main`, and that is a fact, not a bug to debug here

`rope-2026-09-30.md:275-277` says so in its own words: *"**Not wired to the
trainer's config or CLI.** The flag is on `KdaConfig` … wiring it into
`dormouse-core`'s `DormouseConfig` and a CLI flag is the owner's call about
§5."* `grep -i rope` over `schema.rs` and `cfg.rs` on `main` returns nothing
model-shaped. So `--set use_kda_rope=true` on `main` is an **unknown config
key**, which `config/override.rs` refuses loudly. Per the brief's own rule
("if an arm refuses to run, record the refusal LOUDLY and skip") this is
recorded as a refusal, and no code is touched: wiring a flag is a code change
to another lane's crate, not a dispatcher action. It is also the *one* row the
protocol says to run **as a post-training gate, not a BPB race** (AB row 8), so
it was never going to be decided by this wave.

### 2.2 MoE — top-1 of 3, and why not 4

The arm's own fixture and its headline number are 4 experts / top-1
(`moe-routing-2026-10-01.md:1`, "dense shared FFN vs 4× top-1 at equal ACTIVE
FFN FLOPs"), and `small` ships **`n_experts = 3`**. Raising `n_experts` to 4 to
match would add a fourth expert's worth of parameters and turn a
param-neutral routing A/B into a capacity A/B, so the wave runs the shipped
`n_experts = 3` with `--set moe_topk=1`: **top-1 of 3**, still param-neutral
(`num_params` equal at `moe_topk` 0 and 1 is the crate's own gate), which is the
property that makes it comparable to the control at all. `moe_lb_coef` stays
**0.0** — the report ships no coefficient ("no coefficient is shipped, and
`moe_lb_coef` stays 0.0 with the arm off"), and `lb > 0` with routing off is
refused.

**Stated limit on this arm's evidence:** routing is the one arm here whose
on-position does not change `params=`, and `probe::MOE_ROUTE` is **not on the
log line** (only `fb=` and the legacy seams are). So for MoE, "the arm ran" is
carried by the resolved-config snapshot plus the crate's own
`off_is_the_dense_blend_bit_for_bit` gate, not by a runtime counter. A silently
un-engaged MoE run would be indistinguishable from the control in the log. That
is a §1.1 (SILENT) gap in the log line, not in the model — listed in §7.

## 3. The 100k production run — final

**It finished at 14:42 on its own, with nothing done to it.** Final line, verbatim:

```
done steps=100000 best ce=2.415 | BEST HELD-OUT bpb=5.545 at step 86000 -> checkpoints/first_run_100000--retract-every-4-.best.bin
```

| | |
|---|---|
| steps | 100 000, complete (planned 100 000) |
| wall clock | 2026-10-01 02:02 → 14:42 = **12 h 40 m**, ~456 ms/step averaged; warm steps read 463-515 ms |
| **BEST HELD-OUT bpb** | **5.5452847 at step 86 000** (sidecar `….best.bpb`, agrees with the log line) |
| final eval | 5.729 at step 99 500 |
| best train ce | 2.415 |
| NaN / firewall / panic / guard-restart | **0 lines** of all four in 2 126 log lines |
| window | 81 920 B fixed, 200 evals |
| commit | the release binary was built **09-30 18:31, off `a7c3cad`** — which is *before* all six arm landings, so this run provably contains none of them |
| board | `python3 tools/trainboard.py --out /tmp/opencode/board-final ~/logs/first_run_100000_1001_0202.log` → 4 PNGs + index, regenerated 14:44 |
| history | one row appended to `benches/history.tsv` (2026-10-01T11:43:00Z, commit `a7c3cad`) |

**Reading it honestly.** The curve descends 6.480 @ 500 → 6.042 @ 40 500 → 5.769 @
50 500 → 5.720 @ 75 500 → 5.603 @ 85 500 → **5.545 @ 86 000**, and then it does
not descend again: 5.595, 5.603, 5.666, 5.692, 5.740 for the last 14 k steps.
**So the number is best-of-a-plateau, not a late win**, and the last 14 k steps
(11 % of the run, 1 h 45 m) bought nothing — that is the same shape as §3.1's
4.997 row, and the same honest reading applies. Against the 2k controls
(6.343 aux-ON / 6.425 pure-CE, §0.1) the 100k run is **0.80 BPB better**, so the
budget itself is worth ~0.8 BPB over 50× the steps.

The board's second trace makes the regime visible: at step 100 000 the **train**
BPB is ~3.7 and the **held-out** BPB is 5.73. A 2.0-BPB train/held-out gap on a
held-out set of 81 920 B is §2.3's overfit class, not a measurement artefact —
which is also why the last 14 k steps are flat: the model is spending them
fitting bytes it has already seen. **The next budget question is therefore not
"more steps"** (the curve says no) **but "more distinct bytes"** (queue row 3,
working set) **or a larger model** (the owner's ×10 gate, `.bulba/goal.md`).

Two counter readings from the same log, because they are on the line and a
reader will trip over them:

* `fused kda=398012/0 bwd=0 … declined=2037796 ops=811936 node_bwd=0`. The
  **fused forward ran 398 012 times and its result is discarded**; the gradient
  path was the ops arm every step. That is §3.3's FINDING 2 (ADR-0019's silent
  shape: a run that launches a kernel and throws the answer away), and this run
  is another instance of it, not a new defect.
* `norm=0/911936`: the fused RMSNorm kernel was **asked 911 936 times and ran
  zero** — the reachability story of §3.3, unchanged, on a 100k-step run.
