## What landed

Three rare diagnostic series on the eval line, plus the comment fix. Cadence:
eval steps only (default every 500). Every number is a host-side read of
already-materialised factors at a boundary the run already syncs on — no new
device sync was introduced (§1.3).

### (a) Gram error of the FP masters, BEFORE and AFTER retraction

**No new metric.** `LinearLike::max_ortho` already *is* the per-entry masters
reading (`‖UᵀU−I‖_F / rank`, AGENTS §2.3), so the pair is that function called
either side of `retract_tsct*` in `train_loop`. Ladder rung 2: it already
existed in this codebase.

### (b) Gram error of the EFFECTIVE FORWARD factors at the current `alpha`

`dormouse_spectral::ortho_error_forward(u, alpha)` =
`ortho_error_per_entry(ste_ternary_annealed(u, alpha))` — **the same function
`SpectralLinear::forward` calls**, at the layer's own `alpha`. Computed on the
ternary-shaped effective factor, never on the raw master, which is precisely
the metric that would look perfect while the forward degrades.

### (c) The `|s|` spectrum: max, min, count of near-off ranks

`dormouse_spectral::spectrum_stats(s, 1e-3)` → `(max, min, off)`. `off` counts
`|s_i| < 1e-3` ("почти выключенные ранги"), summed across every factor in the
model. 1e-3 is ~3 orders below the per-entry magnitude of a `[768,64]`
orthonormal factor (~3.6e-2); it is a reporting threshold, nothing branches on
it.

## THE MEASUREMENT, and why it matters

On a freshly built `[768,64]` TSCT factor (fp32, CPU, rank 64 — the trainer's
shape), measured with the new functions:

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| masters on the manifold (`‖UᵀU−I‖_F/64`) | **2.27e-7** |
| forward factor at `alpha = 0` | **2.27e-7** (identical — the cross-check) |
| forward factor at `alpha = 0.25` | **1.64e-2** |
| forward factor at `alpha = 0.5` | **3.08e-2** |
| forward factor at `alpha = 0.75` | **4.33e-2** |
| forward factor at `alpha = 1` (the shipped default) | **5.39e-2** |

And on a real `SpectralLinear::new(768, 768, 64)` layer:

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| masters | **8.06e-8** |
| forward at `alpha = 1` | **7.96e-2** |
| forward at `alpha = 0` | **8.06e-8** |
| `\|s\|` at init | max 1.0, min 1.0, off 0 |

**The gap is ~10⁶ and it is the reviewer's warning, confirmed.** The masters
are on the manifold to fp32 precision; the factor the forward actually
multiplies by is 5-8 orders of magnitude further from it. The ternary
projection with its `0.7·mean` dead zone is **not an orthogonal map**, and
`max_ortho` — the only metric the trainer had — cannot see that by
construction: it is a function of the masters alone.

Two consequences that are facts about tonight's run, not forecasts:

1. **The trainer's `max_ortho` 1e-3 latch cannot see this.** It reads 8e-8 on
   a factor whose forward factor is at 8e-2, so the latch never fires and the
   degradation it was installed to catch is invisible to it. The latch's
   threshold is not wrong for what it measures (master drift) — it is just not
   a forward-health metric, and nothing in the log claimed it was. **The live
   2-step run above is the same thing at step 1**: `aft = 2.12e-8` (latch
   quiet) against `fwd = 6.59e-2` (66× over the latch's own threshold).
2. **`alpha` is 1.0 everywhere and nothing in the repo sets it** (`grep
   set_alpha` → only `dormouse-spectral`'s own `SpectralLinear::forward` and its
   diag example). So today's 100k run is in the `alpha = 1` column: the worst
   one, and the column the metric now reports. Whether that 8e-2 per-entry
   Gram error hurts BPB is exactly what the pre-100k A/B (checklist item 3) is
   for; **this lane measures it, it does not decide it.**

## The eval-line field

```text
tsct=<bef>/<aft>/<fwd>/<smin>/<smax>/<off>@a=<alpha>
```

- `bef`/`aft` — masters' per-entry Gram error either side of THIS step's
  retraction. `-` when `retract_every` did not fire on the eval step (two
  identical numbers printed as a pair would read as a measurement and mean "we
  did not measure it" — the same reasoning that put names into the `kda_asked`
  destructuring).
- `fwd` — forward-factor Gram error, worst over factors, at the layer's alpha.
- `smin:smax` — the `|s|` spectrum's range over every factor.
- `off` — near-off rank count, summed.
- `a=` — the largest alpha folded in. `fwd` is a function of alpha, so a
  number without it is not comparable across runs.
- `max`/`sum` conventions match `max_ortho` (worst-case) except `s_min`, which
  is a min because a min is what "a rank died" looks like, and `off`, which is
  a sum because one dead rank in one factor is the fact being reported.

`None` (no field at all) when the model has no TSCT factors — a
`--set use_tsct=false` run prints nothing rather than a row of zeros.

### A REAL eval line (2-batch CPU run, `--preset nano`, 2 steps)

```text
step      1 EVAL ce=5.423 bpb=7.824 BEST over 256 B (fixed window) fused kda=0/0 asked=0 bwd=0 declined=0 ops=0 node_bwd=0 norm=0/0 muon_skipped=0/0 engram=8/8 fb=0/0 tsct=6.90e-6/2.12e-8/6.59e-2/1.000e0/1.000e0/0@a=1.00
```

Read the `tsct=` field left to right:

| slot | value | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| `bef` | **6.90e-6** | masters' per-entry Gram error BEFORE this step's retraction |
| `aft` | **2.12e-8** | …and AFTER: the retraction does its job, 325× better |
| `fwd` | **6.59e-2** | the factor the forward actually multiplies by |
| `smin:smax` | 1.000e0 : 1.000e0 | `s` is all-ones at step 1, as initialised |
| `off` | 0 | no dead ranks yet |
| `a` | 1.00 | the annealing `alpha` the `fwd` number is at |

**This is the whole finding in one line.** The retraction is doing its job
(`aft` is 325× better than `bef`) and the masters are then *held* at 2.12e-8,
far under the 1e-3 latch — while the factor the forward multiplies by reads
**6.59e-2, which is 66× OVER that same latch's threshold.** The latch cannot
fire: it is a function of `bef`/`aft` alone, and those are the two healthy
numbers. Nothing in the log before this field could have told a reader that
the deployed factor is 66× past a limit the model is nominally inside.

## Item 1: the 1.05 comment

`dormouse-spectral`'s `POWER_ITERS` doc and both `mul_scalar(1.05)` sites claimed
1.05 was a safety factor covering the power-iteration error, and the old
comment justified it as "converges from below, so the true `sigma_max` stays
inside the basin". **False as measured** (2026-09-30, AGENTS §3.7 tail): over
200 random `[768,64]` factors the estimate is worse than 5 % on **32–47 %** of
draws and the prescaled `sigma_max` reached **1.087**.

Rewritten to what is true, with the arithmetic:

1. **Cubic suppression.** Expanding about `s = 1`,
   `p(1+e) − 1 = (5/2)·e³ + (15/8)·e⁴ + (3/8)·e⁵` — linear and quadratic terms
   cancel exactly (`p′(1) = 0`), so error is damped cubically. This is why
   the mis-estimate is benign: measured prescaled values up to 1.087 still
   return `σ = 1.000000000`.
2. **The basin, which is the real property.** `p(s) − s = 0` at `s² = 1` and
   `s² = 7/3`, sign negative between, so the attracting interval is
   `0 < x₀ < √(7/3) ≈ 1.5275`. A 1.05 prescale therefore tolerates an estimate
   as bad as `1.5275/1.05 = 0.687` — a **31 % underestimate** — i.e. **6× the
   error the factor was documented as covering**.
3. `POWER_ITERS` bounds the *estimate*, not the factor; it is `p′(1) = 0` that
   absorbs the residue.

The literal `1.05` is now `const SIGMA_OVERSHOOT` (three call sites), so the
comment and the arithmetic cannot drift apart again.

**Why `c*` is not learned** (stated in the comment, because "just make it
learnable" is the obvious wrong next move): `c` multiplies the whole factor, so
a gradient on it is a **gain-control mechanism** — the optimizer would shrink
the factor to cut the loss, which is the exact thing the retraction exists to
prevent. `c* = argmin_c max_{x∈[ℓ,u]} |p³(x/c) − 1|` is measurable offline from
saved factors if the margin ever stops being enough.

## Tests (all red→green by perturbation, not by literal)

| `ortho_per_entry_reports_a_known_perturbation` | fixture `[768,64]` with `diag(1+eps)` on the first `k` rows ⇒ Gram error **exactly** `((1+eps)²−1)/√k` in closed form. `eps = 0` must read < 1e-7; `eps = 0.05` must read **1.281248e-2** vs closed form **1.281248e-2**. An unperturbed read cannot produce it. |
| `ortho_per_entry_is_zero_on_the_manifold` | a retraction applied to an already-orthonormal factor must not report drift |
| `ortho_forward_departures_from_the_masters_as_alpha_rises` | three properties: at `alpha = 0` **exactly** equals the masters' metric to 1e-12; at `alpha = 1` is **> 100×** it; monotone in between. Plus `assert_eq!` that the masters' metric returns the SAME number for any alpha — that is the defect this series exists to expose. |
| `spectrum_stats_reports_max_min_and_dead_ranks` | fixture `(2.0, 1e-4, −0.5, 0.0, 1.25)` ⇒ `(2.0, 0.0, 2)`; tolerance moves only the count (1 / 2 / 3 at 1e-4 / 1e-3 / 1.0); an all-alive spectrum reads 0, so a constant-returning metric fails |

Why the perturbation fixture is built rather than applied to a real factor:
perturbing an existing orthonormal `U` by `ε` moves the *off-diagonal* Gram
entries by `ε·U_ij`, so the expected value would depend on the fixture's own
cross terms instead of on the measurement. The `diag(1+eps)` fixture has none,
which is what makes the expected number a property of the METRIC.

## Carried, not fixed (other lanes / owner decisions)

- **`max_ortho`'s 1e-3 latch is not a forward-health metric**, per the
  measurement above. Changing the latch to fire on `fwd` is a POLICY change
  with a one-way fallback attached and belongs to the owner, not this lane.
  The field now reports both so the decision can be made from a log.
- **`alpha` has no schedule.** Nothing in the repo sets it; the 100k run is
  at `alpha = 1`. Annealing `alpha` is checklist item 3's business.
- **A train at `alpha = 1` runs the 8e-2 forward factor today.** That is a
  measurement, not a verdict: whether it costs BPB is the A/B.
- **Under a non-Fp32 `--quant` (the sm_120 auto default is Fp8), `fwd`
  reports the TERNARY arm, not the deployed one** — `LinearLike::forward`
  takes `forward_quant` (`quant_factor(master)`) whenever
  `quant != Fp32`, and the ternary factor is not what it multiplies.
  Named here rather than silently averaged in. Follow-up if it matters:
  fold `quant_format` into the walk and report both.
- Checklist item 3 (intervals 4 / 16 / none, ≥2 seeds, BPB on equal bytes AND
  equal wall-clock) and item 4 are untouched — they are runs, not code.
