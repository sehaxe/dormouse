# Schedule-free Muon — does it exist, and what of it survives our port?

**Date:** 2026-10-16. **Read-only:** `docs/research/` only; no code, no config, nothing committed.
**Ask:** the owner will not accept a schedule-free AdamW — they want it for **Muon**, our primary
optimizer. Does a schedule-free (or warmup-free / LR-schedule-free) Muon exist, and if not, is the
minimal derivation sound?
**Method:** arXiv MCP, 14 queries across 6 angles (SF×Muon, SF theory, Muon theory, schedule
elimination, clipping/stability, NorMuon), every candidate id resolved with `get_abstract`; two
papers read by section (AMUSE `2605.22432` §5/§9/§10; SF-NorMuon `2605.23061` §3.1/§3.2); one
citation graph. Repo read: `src/dormouse/train/optim.py` (our Muon), `src/dormouse/train/lr.py`
(WSD), `AGENTS.md` §2.3, `docs/protocols/AB-PROTOCOL.md`, `docs/research/2026-10-16-new-tech-scan.md`.
Marks: **[A]** = the paper's own words (abstract or section read) · **[ours]** = repo fact, file named ·
**[S]** = SPECULATION (my inference, no source). Ids verified 2026-10-16.

## TL;DR

1. **It exists, twice, since May 2026 — the baseline scan is wrong about the conclusion, not the window.**
   `2605.22432` AMUSE is schedule-free Muon at **124M/720M/1.3B**, `2605.23061` SF-NorMuon is
   schedule-free *spectral* (NorMuon) at **125M/772M** with a **stationarity theorem** [A].
2. **The scan's line 54 ("no real id found" for schedule-free Muon) is true only of 2026-07..10**, and its
   row 27 dismissal ("SKIP — schedule-free was already evaluated") conflated *schedule-free AdamW* with
   *schedule-free Muon*: `2026-10-07-technology-super-research.md` #3 evaluated **ScheduleFree+ `2605.19095`**,
   an AdamW/LLM result that never names Muon [A].
3. **Nothing newer than 2026-05-21 exists in the home window.** `ti:"schedule-free"` returns 8 papers, newest
   `2607.09167` (theory, Lyapunov analysis of SF-GD/SGD) [A]; no 2026-07..10 SF-Muon appeared.
4. **The transfer of SF theory to a non-gradient update map is already published for the exact-polar case** —
   the SF-Spectral terms (descent + noise + **SF-drift** + **momentum-tracking**) hold with the polar factor
   because polar is the LMO over the spectral-norm ball [A, `2605.23061` Thm 2.1]. **Our port sits outside that
   theorem in three places** (§3): 5-step NS + ColRow instead of `polar()`, **no weight decay on the Muon
   group** (`optim.py:234-237`), and a **per-step polar retraction of the TSCT factors** (`AGENTS.md:406`).
5. **Verdict: DERIVE** — adopt AMUSE's shape as the arm template, not its numbers; gate the three port-specific
   mechanisms before the BPB is believed. The 2k-step A/B below is likely to **tie**, and a tie there is not a
   refutation: the published SF gains are stated at ≥1000 tokens/param [A, `2605.19095`].

## 3. Which part of the SF theory transfers, and which does not (STEP 3)

**Transfers [A, `2605.23061` §3.1].** The SF-Spectral theorem is *already* a theorem about a non-gradient
update map: the polar factor is the linear minimization oracle over the spectral-norm ball, so it pairs with
the gradient through the **nuclear norm** (`⟨G, polar(M)⟩` large ⟺ `‖G‖_*` large), and the proof never needs
the update to equal `−∇f`. It needs only a **descent inequality at `Y_t`**, a **bounded update norm** (ours:
`‖O‖_F ≈ √r` after Normalize_d — exactly what `colrow_normalize` fixes), and a **bounded diameter**. The
momentum buffer and the averaging each cost an explicit term *inside* the bound [A: *"the third term is the
price of schedule-free drift … the explicit momentum … at the cost of a tracking error"*]. So: **yes, iterate
averaging survives a non-gradient update map, in print, for the exact-polar case.**

**Does not transfer — three port-specific gaps, each a gate, not a sentence:**

- **S1 · our update is not `polar(M)`.** `optim.py:116` computes `colrow_normalize(zeropower_via_ns(buf, 5))`
  — the Jordan quintic, **5 steps**, then columns-then-rows normalization (`optim.py:80-84`); SF-NorMuon's
  Algorithm 1 line 7 is `P_t ← polar(M_t)` [A]. Finite-step-NS Muon has its own analyses (`2601.19156`,
  `2609.39595`, `2610.03306`, `2609.33152`) and SF averaging has its own (`2508.06743`, `2607.09167`,
  `2411.07061`) [A] — **but no published theorem composes the two.** [S]: the composition almost certainly
  keeps order `Õ(T^{−1/4})` at a worse constant; that is my inference, not a citation.
- **S2 · our Muon group has NO weight decay.** `optim.py:234-237` builds `Muon(muon_params, lr, momentum,
  ns_steps)`; only the AdamW group gets `weight_decay=wd` [ours]. SF-NorMuon: *"weight decay at the fast iterate
  `Z_t` … is necessary for long-horizon training with schedule-free methods"*, and Lemma 3.1 is what **lifts the
  bounded-diameter assumption** [A]. Our arm as the code stands runs outside the published assumption set —
  gate it with decay at `Z`, or the paper's algorithmically permitted norm-bounded variant.
- **S3 · our TSCT factors are retracted onto the Stiefel manifold every step.** `AGENTS.md:406`:
  *"`train_loop` calls `model.retract_tsct(cfg.retract_iters)` every step"*, with the per-entry `max_ortho`
  latch at 1e-3 [ours]; TSCT factors are Muon-routed (`optim.py:5-8`) [ours]. The per-step map is therefore
  `R(W − η·O(M))` with `R` the polar retraction, and the **averaged iterate `x` is a convex combination of
  retracted iterates — not on the manifold**: for orthonormal `U₁,U₂`, `(1−c)U₁+cU₂` has column norms ≤ 1
  with equality only when `U₁=U₂`. (a) The eval forward at `x` feeds a **non-orthonormal** `U,V`: the
  effective weight `U diag(s) Vᵀ` stays continuous and well-defined, so `x` is a legal parameter point, but
  the orthonormality contract the latch and the factor quantization rely on does not hold there. (b) `R` is
  the closest-point projection onto a **non-convex** set, and the projection step in Lyapunov/Fejér arguments
  needs convexity. **[S] — no published theorem covers SF averaging composed with a retraction**; I expect the
  *empirical* river mechanism (evaluate where oscillation is small, report the average) to be unaffected while
  the *proof* is unavailable for the retracted block.

**The minimal sound recipe for this port (no code written):**

1. Keep model params at `y` (forward, the `retract_tsct` call site and the firewall stay where they are) and
   add **one extra buffer `z`** over the Muon-routed params only — AMUSE §5.4 does exactly this and reports
   **no extra memory versus AdamW** [A].
2. `y_t = (1−β_t) z_t + β_t x_t`, `β_t = 1 − (T₀/t)^ρ (1−β_init)`, `β_init = 0.6`, `ρ = 0.8`, `T₀` = the
   existing warmup token count [A, `2605.22432` Eq. 5].
3. Gradient at `y` → the **existing** momentum buffer (`µ=0.95`) → `NS(5)` → `ColRow` (unchanged) →
   `z ← z − η·scale·O`, **`η` constant, no decay phase** [A].
4. `x_t` materialized only for evaluation: lerp `y,z` in place, eval, restore [A, §5.4] — and **`retract_tsct`
   on `y` only**, while **logging `x`'s factor column-norm deficit** (`‖UᵀU−I‖_F/rank`) every eval: that one
   number decides whether `x` is a model on the same manifold family as `y`.
5. Everything 1-D (norms, `s` scales, iteration embeddings, Engram tables) stays on the current AdamW
   (or SF-AdamW) path; **gate `S2`** (decay or norm bound at `z`) before the arm claims the theorem.

**Failure modes to gate before believing a BPB:** a `β_t` bug leaves `y≈z` — silent, the arm *becomes*
constant-LR Muon and looks merely mediocre; masked NaN steps shrink the effective step count and SF's `c_t` is
**step-indexed**, so the average is not the average of the run it thinks it ran; the `max_ortho` latch is
one-way and persists in the checkpoint [ours, `AGENTS.md:409`], so record its step-0 state and refuse comparison
if it flips; and `x`-only wins must be reported as `x`-only wins.

## 4. A/B design (STEP 4) — if derived

**Protocol.** `small`, pure CE (`--jepa-weight 0 --dspark-weight 0`), **one batch size and one `seq_len`
across all arms and seeds** (window = `eval_batches × batch × seq_len`, `AB-PROTOCOL.md:37`) [ours]; fixed
operating depth; **3 seeds per arm**; 2000 steps; verdict metric = the **BPB curve and the `x`↔`y` gap**, not
the endpoint (a horizon-dependent mechanism judged at one endpoint is the mistake `2609.04577` names) [A].

**Arms (3):** **A0** = WSD control (live recipe, `lr.py:20-48`); **A1** = **constant LR, no averaging** (same
warmup, no decay) — the cheapest null, and the one AMUSE's Figure 6 says loses badly [A: *"constant learning
rate Muon benefits significantly from both EWA and learning rate decay"*]; **A2** = constant LR + SF averaging
per §3, returning `x`. Without **A1** the arm confounds "dropped the schedule" with "added the average" —
one-factor discipline, `AB-PROTOCOL.md:389` [ours].

**Void-check counters (read before any verdict):** (a) **byte count identical on every eval line** of all nine
runs — otherwise the table is void regardless of the numbers; (b) `fused kda=<f>/<b>` with **b>0** in every arm
(the control precondition) — `kda=<f>/0` is a network whose attention is frozen at init; (c) `muon_skipped=` /
`finalize` present and stable, proving the Muon path ran; (d) **the NaN-firewall skipped-step count = 0** in
every run, for the SF reason above; (e) **one new counter is required** — steps at `y`, `x` materializations,
reverts — because a schedule-free arm whose `x` was never materialized is the `8fa5d4c` failure class, and only a
counter proves the arm ran. No code is edited here; the design lists it.

**Pre-registered prediction [S]:** `A2 ≲ A1 ≲ A0` on the held-out BPB **curve**, with the largest gap at the end
of the run. I predict the 2000-step endpoint **ties** (published SF gains are stated at ≥1000 tokens/param
[A, `2605.19095`]; 2k steps × batch × 512 bytes is orders below that), and that a tie will be **misread as a
refutation**. Decision rule: A2 wins iff it beats A1 by more than the pooled A0/A1 seed spread on any window;
**a tie at 2k escalates to one 10×-step arm before deletion**, which is the only reading that separates
"SF does not work here" from "we measured the wrong horizon".

## 7. Open questions

  `AGENTS.md:409`)? Only our own measurement answers it — and it decides whether `x` needs its own retraction.
