## TL;DR — six verdicts

1. **The newest credible thing is round-level *inheritance* gating, not task
   selection.** 2610.22029 (10-15, `(F)`) audits R-Zero/Agent0/Skill Self-Play:
   **42-52% of native Agent0/R-Zero admissions leave the frontier under
   independent responses**, **60 of 96 Skill-SP updates have nonpositive
   correct-target NLL gain**. HeirCommit (freeze the successor, compare against
   root on *fresh* probes, inherit or retain) **cuts harmful accepted updates by
   68-83%**; EvoHeir raises the base model's math average **7.9-30.1%** over four
   backbones. This is the thing to build.
2. **The gate beats the reward.** 2605.22217 (`(F)`, controlled, with a
   deterministic-DSL twin): **every gate-off configuration collapses regardless
   of reward design; every gate-on configuration learns.** The gate is "executes
   deterministically, same output twice". Cheaper than every reward fix in the
   2026 literature, and it is the binding constraint.
3. **The only byte-native self-play loop found is 2609.30063** (`(F)`, 09-24): a
   generator proposes programs for a universal Turing machine, the machine emits
   **byte sequences** (vocab 256, random init), the learner does next-byte CE,
   the generator gets a **preconditioned gradient-alignment reward**. Zero-shot
   loss scales as a power law in compute across text/images/music/DNA — in
   **bits per byte**, our own metric.
4. **Anchoring has three independent 2026 shapes**, all cheap: a frozen
   **previous-iteration model** (2610.09856, +2.5% math / +2.8% general over
   Agent0), a **running-average reference** (2606.22688), and a **flat-top
   difficulty band** on the task's solver win rate (2608.19197, band [0.4, 0.6]).
   Our 10-15 anchor (2607.03523) is the first kind; the band is the one we lack.
5. **Self-rewarding / LLM-judge self-play is measured broken, not merely
   suspect.** 2607.05904 (`(A)`): on GSM8K with Qwen3, self-play drives the
   judge's pass rate **0.72 → 0.94 while true accuracy stays 0.20** (3 seeds);
   the fix that works is a judge that **commits an answer first** (false positives
   0.719 → 0.012). At 57M active the judge is the network being trained.
6. **Build order:** (i) determinism gate + difficulty band on the existing
   `tasks.py`/`verifiers.py` loop; (ii) HeirCommit round gating + experience
   archive; (iii) byte-level generator/learner self-play as a *pretraining* lane,
   only after (i)-(ii) show a measurable probe-set gain.

## 2. Top-3 expanded — the loop, not the idea

### 2.1 HeirCommit + EvoHeir (2610.22029) — the round gate

**Generates:** nothing new. It wraps whatever generator we already run (a
proposer, or a mutation of `tasks.py`). That is why it is #1: it composes with
every other row. One round, per the paper's §1 and Figure 1:

1. **Explore** — run the round's proposer/solver loop → a candidate successor
   `M′` plus the round's verified tasks and solutions.
2. **Screen** — compare `M′` against the current model (the *root*) on **fresh
   probes** neither proposer nor solver has seen. The paper's argument: reusing
   selection evidence to accept a branch pays a union bound over branches; fresh
   confirmation removes it.
3. **Recover** (EvoHeir) — repair `M′`'s failures from **current and archived
   reference solutions** while preserving its successes. References from **8 of
   10 donors weaker than the current model still improved the recipient**.
4. **Confirm** — commit `M′` only if the *post-absorption* state beats root on
   the fresh probes; else **retain root**.
5. **Archive** — qualified experience enters regardless of the parameter
   decision ("parameter inheritance can stop without discarding material that
   helps a later recipient"); rejected-round experience adds **+0.66 pp** beyond
   committed-round history.

**What stops collapse:** a rejection test on the *state*, not the task — harmful
commits −68-83% at equal acceptance rate. The archive stops the loop discarding
learning when the model is rejected.

**Cost (X):** one frozen copy (57M active — trivial), a held-out probe set
(`build_tasks("code", probe_seed)`, never trained), a probe-eval per round, a
JSONL archive. The expensive part is *honest statistics* (§6).

### 2.2 Determinism gate + difficulty band (2605.22217 + 2608.19197 + 2608.21871)

**Generates:** the proposer — in our lane, a mutation operator over `tasks.py`'s
three templates (constants, docstring, test calls) first; a template-writing
policy only if mutation works.

**Filters, in order:** (1) **executability + determinism** (2605.22217's `F_ε`) —
run the proposed task through the verifier harness **twice**, admit only on
identical output; **set ε = 0**. (2) **Difficulty band** (2608.19197's flat-top
anchor, band [0.4, 0.6] solver win rate; equivalently 2608.21871's half-capture
rule) — our plan's "frontier 70% (pass@k 30-60%)" with a measured shape.
(3) **Diversity** (2602.13103) — a persistent memory bank penalizes tasks that
recycle earlier rounds; at our scale (X) exact-duplicate and constant-difference
detection is enough for v1.

**Trains:** the solver on admitted tasks (our CRR/advantage path); the proposer
only if it is a policy — 2608.19197 uses per-role advantage normalization, a
delayed designer update with truncated importance sampling, asymmetric clipping
(0.2/0.28); 2609.32228 replaces the band with gradient alignment.

**What stops collapse:** the gate (gate-off collapses under *every* reward,
including a grounded one), the band (difficulty regulation), the bank (recycling).
Watch the paper's counter-intuitive coupling, the **Grounded Proposer Paradox**:
a proposer with ground-truth access collapses *faster* when paired with a
self-consistency solver, by concentrating training on clean tasks that form the
fastest path to a spurious self-consistent attractor. Do not hand the proposer
the answers.

### 2.3 Byte-level generator/learner self-play (2609.30063) — the pretraining lane

**Generates:** programs. Both models are Llama-arch transformers from **random
init** with **byte-level tokenization, vocab 256** — the port's vocabulary. A
Brainf*ck-like universal Turing machine executes each program with a random
input tape; the **output bytes** are the training data (programs prefixed by byte
`S`, outputs by byte `O`).

1. Sample `N` programs; build the pool `fresh ∪ mutated(high-reward past
   programs) ∪ replay(earlier rounds)`.
2. Execute each → output byte sequences.
3. **Learner:** one gradient step on standard next-byte CE, averaged over the
   pool.
4. **Generator:** a GRPO batch-level policy-gradient step on
   `r_i = |⟨∇_θ L(y_i;θ), P_e ⊙ δθ_e⟩|`, where `δθ_e = θ_{⌊e/2⌋} − θ_e` is the
   learner's parameter movement over a **growing lookback window** and `P_e` is
   the diagonal AdamW preconditioner. Mastered programs give ~zero gradient;
   unlearnable structure gives misaligned gradient; **high reward = not yet
   mastered *and* congruent with recent progress** — a frontier signal with no
   external reward. KL-regularized to the Solomonoff prior
   `g_0(x) = |A|^{-ℓ(x)}`; sequence-level importance ratio clipped to `e^{±20}`;
   plus reward-weighted SFT (expert iteration, λ=1.0) against generator
   forgetting.
5. Repeat: **34.36B tokens** max, context 4096, K seeds ensembled.

The paper explicitly rejects difficulty-based rewards — "a program can be made
arbitrarily difficult without containing useful structure, for example by
injecting random bytes into an otherwise predictable sequence". Collapse is
bounded by the KL to the prior, the mutation+replay pool, and expert iteration.

**Cost (X):** small interpreter; the reward needs a **JVP** (`torch.func.jvp`)
per program per round. Our Rust trainer is launch-bound (AGENTS §3.1: mean GPU
utilisation 13.3%), so many small JVPs suit the Python port, not the trainer.

## 3. Our implementation sketch

All of it in `port/src/dormouse/post/`, reusing what exists: `tasks.build_tasks`,
`verifiers.verify`, `rollouts.rollout` (byte-budgeted), `train_steps.crr_advantage`,
and `train/sft.py`'s `render` / `sft_loss` / `sft_train` (`train/sft.py:399`,
`:586`, `:609`) as the training seam. No new heavy dependency.

| file | what it adds | LOC (X) |
|---|---|---|
| `post/gate.py` | `admit(task, solver) -> bool`: execute twice, require identical verifier output; `epsilon` leak (default 0, COUNTED when non-zero); returns a `GateVerdict` | 70-100 |
| `post/propose.py` | proposer: deterministic mutation of `tasks.py` templates + persistent dedup bank; `propose(cls, seed, round, n) -> list[Task]` | 150-220 |
| `post/selfplay.py` | round orchestrator: propose → gate → band → G solver rollouts/task → `verify` → advantage → `sft_train` step → freeze successor → fresh-probe confirm → commit/retain | 250-350 |
| `post/archive.py` | JSONL store `(task, verified_solution, round, win_rate, kept)` + recovery selection | 80-120 |
| `post/train_steps.py` (edit) | add `anchor_relative_advantage(group, anchor_group)` (2610.09856) and `gmae_advantage(groups)` (2609.36750) beside `crr_advantage` | +60-100 |

**Total ≈ 610-890 LOC (X).**

**First pre-registered experiment** (arms written down before the run; ADR-0002
"a tie deletes the mechanism"):

- **Polygon:** 9.2M `small`, `code` class only (the `exec` verifier is
  deterministic and cheap), 3 seeds per arm, one GPU, one heavy thing at a time.
- **Arms.** **A — control:** masked-CE SFT on the seed task set for the same
  number of learner steps, no proposer, no self-play. **B — gated self-play:**
  proposer + `gate.py` (ε=0) + difficulty band + CRR/group advantage, inheriting
  every round (R-Zero-shaped). **C — B + inheritance gate:** adds `archive.py`
  and the HeirCommit fresh-probe confirm/retain, plus EvoHeir-style recovery.
- **Held fixed:** learner gradient steps, total rollout count, task-generator
  seed, byte budget, wall-clock budget. **Probe set:** `build_tasks("code",
  seed=probe_seed)`, 128 tasks, never trained on, evaluated every round.
- **Metrics:** primary = **verifier pass rate on the probe seed, reported as
  `correct@budget(256 B)`** (the owner's byte-thrift contract); secondary = BPB
  on `eval_tail` at the port's declared window; monitors = cross-round duplicate
  rate (cheap MAP) and the within-input-entropy / cross-input-MI pair from the
  10-15 sweep's collapse checklist.
- **Pre-registered rule:** C > B > A on probe pass rate beyond the control's own
  seed spread, or the mechanism is deleted. B ≥ C ⇒ drop HeirCommit. A ≥ B ⇒ the
  loop earns nothing at 9.2M; stop the lane and say so.

**Byte-level note.** Every method here is tokenizer-agnostic *except* those whose
reward is per token (per-token logprobs, token-level KL, `div_gate`-style
divergence, 2605.07725 from the 10-15 sweep). At byte level those must be
re-defined per **byte**, and byte sequences are 3-4× longer than token
sequences, so a per-position signal costs 3-4× more. The two shapes that survive
unchanged: per-sequence CE (2609.30063) and verifier pass/fail over a
byte-budgeted rollout (`rollouts.py`). Our metric being BPB is a coincidence in
our favour — 2609.30063's own reported quantity is bits per byte.

## 4. Explicit rejections

| rejected | why |
|---|---|
| **Self-rewarding / LLM-judge self-play** (2401.10020, 2605.09959 G-Zero, 2608.26582 J-Zero, 2603.09206 MM-Zero, 2604.20051) | 2607.05904 (`(A)`): judge pass 0.72 → 0.94 at true accuracy 0.20 (GSM8K, Qwen3, 3 seeds); errors transfer across judge families, a 3-judge ensemble still accepts 55%; 2607.07436 (`(S)`) shows a biased judge silently disables skill retirement. | **Unanchored adversarial / competitive self-play** (2607.03523, 2608.01559) | 2607.03523: unanchored generator–fixer self-play drifts to hard-but-unrealistic bugs and *degrades* on human-authored ones. 2608.01559 (`(S)`): controlled negative result — the competitive component gave no gain on legal reasoning. |
| **Debate / multi-agent discussion as the training signal** | No 2026 source found with a measured verifiable-reward gain from LM debate self-play. Nearest: 2606.30571 (`(S)`, attractor states in multi-turn model–model conversations) and 2601.13566 (`(S)`, theory of debate/bootstrap as coherence optimisation). No measured gain ⇒ no build. |
| **Skill libraries / procedural memory** (2607.22529, 2610.14515, 2605.19576, 2607.12790) | All LLM-agent scale. At 57M active a natural-language skill store is (X) unproven, and library drift is a *silent* failure mode (2605.19576) whose only known guard is an unbiased judge we cannot field. Defer. |
| **Population-based multi-adapter self-play** (2605.16727 PopuLoRA) | LoRA teachers/students on a shared **frozen** base; our model trains from scratch on one GPU, so a population multiplies VRAM and wall-clock for an LLM-scale benefit. |
| **Chess / driving / game self-play** (2609.37447, 2610.20866, 2609.21617, 2610.14883, 2610.13995) | Off-domain for code/math post-training. 2609.37447 is a useful efficiency datapoint (6.32M params, one 8-GPU node, 2.5 days) that transfers to nothing here. |
| **Multimodal / safety self-play** (2609.36224 MATE, 2610.15449, 2606.09701, 2610.05346) | Out of scope (vision, red-teaming). |

  10-15 sweep), 30B (SPADE, Explorer-Zero). A byte-level, 57M-active, MoE+Engram
  model is **outside the published evidence**; every row is a transfer.
- **(X) Whether HeirCommit's fresh-probe confirmation resolves at 128 tasks.**
  Our paired-eval resolution is estimated 0.002-0.005 BPB (AGENTS §3.1,
  unverified); the power of a 128-task pass-rate test against a round-to-round
  difference is computed nowhere I read. **Measure it first** — a confirmation
  gate that cannot resolve a difference always passes.
- **(X) The JVP reward is affordable on the port, not on the Rust trainer.** One
  forward-mode JVP per program per round on a 9.2M model is cheap in Python; the
  Rust trainer is launch-bound and would pay per-launch overhead for a
  per-program scalar. I measured neither.
- **(X) A mutation-only proposer may be enough.** All 14 rows use a learned
  proposer; none tests "mutate the existing 3 templates" as a baseline. It is the
  cheapest arm and the one that could make rows 2 and 7-10 unnecessary at our
  scale.
- **(X) The band and the anchor are the same thing** (difficulty regulation) at
  different granularities — the band on task difficulty, the anchor on model
  identity. No paper I found says so.
