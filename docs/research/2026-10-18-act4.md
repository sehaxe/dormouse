## 1. TL;DR

1. **[T] a4.8's recipe is a layer-split, not a group-scale recipe.** 4-bit for the inputs to the
   attention and FFN layers; the intermediate states (before FFN down-projection and attention
   output-projection) are **sparsified (Q-Sparse, TopK 50%) + 8-bit** — the hybrid exists and is
   the paper's headline. "group" and "per-token" do not occur in the paper at all; the INT8 formula
   is `γ = max(|X|)`; full-INT4 uses an **absmean** scale `β = 2·mean(|X|)` for the FFN-down inputs
   [T `2411.04965` §3.1-3.2].
2. **[T] BitNet v2 is the from-scratch validation of the rotation axis**: online Hadamard (activation
   side only) + per-token **absmax for 8-bit / absmean for 4-bit**; at W1.58A4 with no rotation the
   1.3B and 3B runs **diverge**; with rotation, a8 matches b1.58 and a4 degrades "minimally" —
   1.3B/3B/7B trained from scratch [T `2504.18415` §3.1-3.3].
3. **[ours] Our v2_int4 arm diverges from the paper twice**: the port uses per-token **absmax** for
   4-bit where the paper uses **absmean** (`hadamard.py:186-220` says so in its own docstring), and
   the config **refuses v2_* at `d_model=768`** (not 2^k, `config.py:909-922`) — `small` is 768, so
   the v2 arm cannot run on the preset our A/B uses.
4. **[A] The 2026 counter-trend: Hadamard is no longer the automatic answer.** Averis
   (`2603.10444`) beats "NVIDIA's Hadamard-based outlier-smoothing method" in **W4A4G4 FP4 training**
   (Qwen3 0.6B/100B tokens, 7B-A1.5B MoE/50B tokens) by subtracting the **coherent mean** before
   quantizing; UE5M3 (`2609.02846`) **omits RHT entirely** and lowers an 8B/190B-token loss with
   wider (E5M3) block scales.
5. **[A] Stochastic rounding is a gradients/optimizer-states technique, never a forward-activation
   one** in everything surveyed; and `2605.09825` (Llama-3.1-8B pretraining, native FP4) reports SR
   **fails** to stabilize once Wgrad is quantized, while **deterministic** Hadamard restores stability.
6. **[A] Fine-grained scaling for training is block 16-32, not 128**: NVFP4 block-16/E4M3 was
   measured optimal over block sizes and formats (`2505.19115`); MXFP4 is block-32/E8M0;
   `2609.02846` block-16/E5M3; `2607.24953` 2D blocks. **`act_group=128` has no source in the
   activation-training literature** — it is a port convention, not an a4.8 recipe.
7. **[ours] Our A/B cannot resolve any of this**: control spread 0.0124 BPB, all four arms inside it
   (deltas ≤ 0.0044); the literature's effects are quoted at 1.3B-8B. The next A/B should pick the
   axis with the **cheapest faithful arm**, not the axis with the biggest paper number.

## 3. The eight questions, answered

### 3.1 Group scales / fine-grained scaling — `act_group=128` is REFUTED as an a4.8 recipe
[T `2411.04965`]: the INT8 quantizer is `γ/127·RoundClip(127/(γ+ε)·X, −128, 127), γ = max(|X|)`; the
hybrid is **which layers get 4-bit** (Gaussian-like attention/FFN inputs) **vs sparse+8-bit**
(sharp intermediate states); the full-INT4 ablation uses an **absmean** scale `β = 2·mean(|X|)` for
the FFN-down inputs "as its inputs have larger outliers"; training uses a **two-stage 8→4-bit
recipe**. Searches for `group` and `per-token` in `2411.04965` return **zero** passages; `2504.18415`
also has zero `group` passages; `2504.12285` says "absmax… applied per-token" for its **8-bit**
activations [T]. The fine-grained-scaling evidence in *training* is block 16 (NVFP4/E4M3, measured
optimal in `2505.19115`; block-16/E5M3 in `2609.02846`) or 32 (MXFP4/E8M0) or 2D blocks
(`2607.24953`) — never 128. **If the group axis is tested, test 16/32; 128 has no source.**

### 3.2 Rotations — fixed Hadamard is validated for FS training; learned rotations are PTQ-only
Fixed: BitNet v2 (FS, 1.3B-7B; activation-only rotation suffices; no-rotation A4 diverges) [T];
QuEST (FS, W4A4→W1A1) [A]; HALO (fwd+bwd) [A]; AdaHOP (FS MXFP4) [A]; and inside the FP4
pretraining recipes `2607.04422` / `2610.00053` / `2606.20381` [A]. Deterministic > randomized:
`2605.09825` found deterministic Hadamard restores stability where **RHT and SR fail** [A]. Learned:
SpinQuant, ThinQuant, SpectrumQuant, CodeQuant are all **PTQ calibration** [A] — **no from-scratch
training validation of learned rotations was found**. Counter-evidence to Hadamard-as-default:
Averis beats a Hadamard baseline in W4A4G4 training [A]; UE5M3 omits RHT [A].

### 3.3 Outlier migration (SmoothQuant class) in QAT-from-scratch — no source found
SmoothQuant is explicitly **training-free PTQ** with an offline per-channel migration [A]; the
migration presumes frozen weights. Every 2025-26 successor found is PTQ-side (DuQuant++-class,
CodeQuant, SpectrumQuant, ThinQuant). The *training-side* analogues of outlier migration are
mean-split (Averis), Hadamard/RHT, and outlier extraction (AdaHOP). **[S2] I found no paper applying
SmoothQuant-style migration in from-scratch training, and I assume the per-channel scale is not
invariant under a moving weight.**

### 3.4 Learnable clipping / step (LSQ, PACT) — canonical, but 2025-26 LLM training did not pick it up
LSQ `1902.08153` (learnable step size; 2-4 bit, ImageNet) and PACT `1805.06085` (learnable clip α)
are verified [A]. 2025-26 successors found: Max-Window `2605.26189` (scale estimation, DTS,
amax saturation — a *failure mode* of delayed scale estimates, invisible in training loss) [A];
BATQuant `2603.16590` (learnable block-wise, PTQ) [A]; JAB `2609.34866` (STE with learnable scales,
inside a PTQ fitter) [A]. **No 2025-26 result found where LSQ/PACT-style learnable clipping beats
fixed absmax/absmean scales in from-scratch LLM training** — the field moved the difficulty into
rotations/mean-split and format block scales instead. [S1] On our STE graph a learnable scale is a
cheap arm (a parameter per block), but it fights `max_value()`'s "block max lands on the format max"
invariant and needs its own gold test.

### 3.5 Hybrid precision by layer/channel — the split that matters is *which layers*, not attention-vs-FFN bits
a4.8: 4-bit attention/FFN **inputs**, 8-bit **intermediate states** (FS) [T]. BitNet v2: 4-bit all
linears after Hadamard, **BOS KV heads retained at 8-bit**, 3-bit KV otherwise (FS) [T].
`2607.24953`: **MXFP8 for Q/K**, FP4 elsewhere (FS) [A]. `2607.04422`: softmax-sensitive ops in BF16
(FS) [A]. Q-PACE: learned per-layer allocation (FS/QAT, ≤4B) [A]. PTQ-side: MxMoE (linear blocks
vary in sensitivity), `2609.34866` (which matrix matters more than any sensitivity estimate),
HyQuant (sink tokens + local window high) [A]. **Our `attention >= 8` rule is consistent with a4.8
and conservative vs v2/2607.24953 — but in the port it is dormant: `attn_path` has no call site
(grep 2026-10-18), so no quantized seam currently sits inside attention.**

### 3.6 Stochastic rounding for activations — not a forward-pass technique in any source found
SR appears as: backward/update rounding in FQT (`2505.19115`: SR backward+update, **RTN forward**)
[A]; "selective stochastic rounding to backward gradients" (`2609.02846`) [A]; row-gradient SR
(`2610.00053`) [A]; "SR only on dY" (`2606.20381`) [A]; theory vs batch size (`2511.00874`) [A];
survey (`2603.06060`) [A]. And the negative: SR **fails** to stabilize Wgrad in native MXFP4
pretraining (`2605.09825`) [A]. **No source found applies SR to forward activation quantization.**
[S2] I assume the reason is that SR needs a high-precision master to round from, which a forward
activation does not have.

### 3.7 MoE-specific activation quantization — block-level, not expert-input-level
`2603.02731` (671B MoE FS): MXFP4 for **activations and expert-parallel communication**, FP8 for the
core MoE compute; −14.8% peak activation memory (11.8 GB), +12.5% throughput, "without degrading
convergence" [A]. Averis: 7B-A1.5B MoE, W4A4G4 FS [A]. `2607.24953`: 30B MoE FS [A]. UFP4: MoE
7.9B/124B [A]. PTQ-side MoE: MxMoE (divergent expert activation frequencies create heterogeneous
compute) and CodeQuant [A]. **No paper found that quantizes *expert-input* distributions
differently per expert during training** — every training-side MoE paper quantizes the block
uniformly. [S2] Per-expert scale rules are an open, untested axis.

### 3.8 Newer than 2026-06, directly on 4-bit activations in pretraining
`2607.04422` (2026-07-05), `2607.24953` (2026-07), `2609.02846` (2026-09-02), `2610.00053`
(2026-09-04), `2609.33601` (2026-09-27, PTQ/QAT distillation), `2610.09183` (2026-10-06). The
direction of travel: block-scale FP4 (E4M3/E5M3) + outlier handling by Hadamard **or** mean-split +
SR restricted to gradients + attention sub-paths held at higher precision. Nothing newer than
2026-06 changes the ranking of §5.

- **[S2]** The `act_group=128` in our burn transcription came from somewhere I could not find (not
