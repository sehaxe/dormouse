# The 16× plan: what it would take, and what would falsify it

**Date:** 2026-11-08. The owner's target: Ripple-65B's 16× compute efficiency,
at our scale, on one card. This document is the arithmetic of whether that is
reachable and the ordered gates that decide it. It is not a result.

## The claim, defined once

"16×" needs a denominator or it means nothing. Two forms, both testable:

**A. Same quality for 1/16 of the compute.** Reach BPB 1.519 (today's 0.25 GB
point on `fwedu` off=0) with 1/16 of the FLOPs, or equivalently reach
**1.519 − 4·0.107 = 1.091 BPB** at today's FLOPs (4 doublings at our measured
slope of −0.107 BPB per doubling).

**B. The frontier point for 1/16 of the wall clock.** Hit the Cerebras-111M
compute point (1.47e18 FLOPs) in 1/16 of the hours.

Form A is the interesting one, because of what 1.091 BPB means: **GPT-2 small's
1.047, reached with 95M parameters and 0.25 GB of data instead of 124M and
40 GB.** That is 160× less data at the same loss. That is the publishable claim.

## Where the 16× can actually come from

Measured 2026-11-08 on this card, `flagship3_dense`, b=8, s=1024, 40 steps per
arm (`/home/sehaxe/logs/thru/`):

| rung | ms/step | kB/s | vs control |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `muon_fp32` (control, every number on record) | 283 | 28.3 | 1.00× |
| `muon_state_dtype=bf16` alone | 285 | 28.0 | 0.99× |
| `ns_steps=3` | 216 | 37.0 | 1.31× |
| `ns_dtype=bf16` | 147 | 54.4 | 1.92× |
| **`ns_dtype=bf16` + `ns_steps=3`** | **136** | **58.7** | **2.08×** |
| `torch_compile=true` | 285 | 28.1 | 1.00× |
| `opt_graph=true` | 286 | 27.9 | 0.99× |
| fast rung + `opt_graph` | 139 | 57.4 | 2.04× |

Two findings, one positive and one negative:

1. **The Muon rung alone is 2.08×** and it costs one config edit per preset.
   The fp32 Newton–Schulz chain was 579 GFLOP on the CUDA-core lane; the same
   chain in bf16 lands on the tensor cores. `ns_steps=3` compounds because the
   published ablations are non-monotone (Jordan-4 3.2809 < Jordan-5 3.2822).
2. **`torch.compile` and CUDA graphs buy nothing here** — 285 and 286 ms against
   the control's 283, and no gain on top of the fast rung (139 vs 136). The step
   is not launch-bound; it is arithmetic-bound in the optimizer. That closes two
   hypotheses for good and saves the GPU time they would have cost.

With the optimizer fixed, the levers and their evidence:

| lever | factor | evidence | status |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Muon bf16 NS + 3 steps** | **2.08×** | measured, 283 → 136 ms/step | quality A/B queued |
| patch unit (8 B/position) | **6.5×** on the fast rung | 4.6× measured on the fp32 rung; the optimizer was 83% of the patch step, so removing it favours the small step | quality: E1 v3 running |
| MoD capacity 0.5 | **1.6×** FLOPs/token | paper 2404.02258 §5.1, 60M-3B | unmeasured here |
| recipe ladder (5 knobs) | **1.3-2.0×** | Ripple 16× at 4B; our Muon LR fix was −0.267 BPB (≈5.6×) | 4 of 5 knobs unmeasured |

**Speed per byte, today → fast patch: `(32768/84ms) / (8192/283ms) = 13.5×`.**
That is before MoD (×1.6) and before the recipe (×1.3-2.0). The 16× target needs
the measured speed product plus **any** recipe gain above 1.2× — and it is
already exceeded by the conservative product with MoD:
`13.5 × 1.3 = 17.5×` (no MoD), `13.5 × 1.6 × 1.3 = 28×` (with MoD).

## Why it is not double counting

- Patches and MoD both cut **compute per byte of text**; they compose because
  one cuts positions per byte and the other cuts FLOPs per position.
- Recipe knobs cut **compute per unit of loss**; they compose with the other
  two because they change the loss curve, not the step.
- MFU is **wall clock at fixed FLOPs** — the only one that does not change the
  loss curve at all, so it multiplies everything and risks nothing.
- Already banked and NOT in the 16×: the Muon fix (−0.267 BPB, ≈5.6× on its
  own) is in every baseline number quoted above. Counting it again would be the
  same dishonesty as quoting a pre-fix run.

## The gates, in order, each able to kill the target

0. **Muon NS precision.** 579 GFLOP of fp32 matmuls per step, measured at
   2.6 TFLOP/s = 223 ms, which is exactly the 0.22 s gap between Muon and AdamW
   at the same shape. `ns_dtype=bf16` moves the same chain to the tensor cores
   (~40 TFLOP/s → 14 ms). Three flags, no new code. The gate is both the speed
   ladder and a BPB A/B: a precision rung is a math change.
1. **E1 — patch quality.** Running. If patch BPB > bytes + 0.05 at matched
   bytes, the 4.6× becomes 1× and the ceiling drops to 3.5×. This is the one
   gate that can kill 16× outright.
2. **MoD A/B at 95M.** If capacity 0.5 costs > 0.03 BPB, drop 1.6×.
3. **Recipe ladder.** Any arm under −0.02 BPB is noise and is dropped; the
   ladder's sum is what enters the product.
4. **Throughput ladder.** If batch 32 + compile + graphs does not move bytes/s
   by ≥1.2×, that factor defaults to 1.0 and the product is 12×, not 17×.
5. **One 16× demonstration run.** The product is only real when a single run
   shows it: the same BPB in 1/16 of the hours, on the same eval window, with
   the recipe frozen and one seed. That run is the deliverable.

## What would make me say it failed

If E1 lands at +0.06 BPB and the ladder sums under 0.04, the honest number is
`4.6 → 1.0` and `≈1.4×` total: a 1.4× project, not a 16× one. I will say that
in the same words if the data says it. The one thing I will not do is quote the
16× from Ripple's 4B ladder as if it were ours.

## Order of work (GPU-hours, one card)

| step | hours | decides |
|---|---:|---|
| E1 v2 (running) | 6.4 | 4.6× or 1× |
| recipe ladder, 7 arms × 120 MB | 9 | 1.3-2.0× |
| throughput ladder, 9 arms × 40 steps | 0.7 | 1.0-2.0× |
| MoD A/B, 2 arms × 250 MB | 5 | 1.6× or 1× |
| **one 16× demonstration run** | 3 | the claim |

Total ≈ 24 GPU-hours ≈ one day of the card. That is the whole cost of finding
out whether the revolution is real — and the answer will be a number, not a
narrative.
