[1]The optimizer was 70% of the step

    

      Muon's Newton–Schulz chain ran 579 GFLOP of fp32 matmuls per step on the
      CUDA-core lane — 223 ms of a 307 ms step, measured against AdamW at the
      same shape. Moving the chain to bf16 puts it on the tensor cores. Same
      polynomial, same direction, different silicon.

  • 
        
    “16× from the recipe ladder”
    
        
    1.89× from the optimizer rung, measured
    
        
    The 16× was Ripple-65B's result at 4B parameters, not ours. Our own recipe knobs are still unmeasured; the step-speed win that is measured belongs to bf16 Newton–Schulz.
    
      
    • 
      
  • 
        
    “3 Newton–Schulz steps are a tie”
    
        
    +0.27 BPB at [832, 3072]
    
        
    The published non-monotone result, and this repo's own “1.17e-15 from the manifold”, were both measured on rank-64 factors. On the dense stack's shapes the 3-step chain does not converge, and a non-orthogonal Muon update is a badly scaled momentum step. Two arms, two precisions, both red.
    
      
    • 
      
  • 
        
    “209× less compute than GPT-2”
    
        
    53×, and only per byte
    
        
    The first figure counted GPT-2's 40 GB of text as 40 G positions; at ~4 bytes per BPE token it is 10 G positions. The corrected ratio is still large, but it is a ratio of compute per byte, not of capability.
    
      
    • 
      
  • 
        
    “1.927 BPB at 98 MB”
    
        
    1.660 BPB at the same bytes
    
        
    The first curve was captured before the Muon learning-rate fix (a scheduler overwrote the group's LR every step). The corrected curve is 1.660 → 1.519 BPB, a slope of −0.107 per doubling.
