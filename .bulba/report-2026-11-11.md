# Night report — 2026-11-11

Goal for the night (owner's framing): **highest intelligence density** — minimum
shipped bytes, minimum inference FLOPs, minimum training data/compute, maximum
quality — with the rule *never learn what you cannot keep* (no compress-a-smart-
model-later).

The single question that decides it: **does the low-rank factorisation (TSCT)
hold quality at a scale that matters?** Our only prior number was +0.04 BPB at
9.2M (meaningless at that size), and the direct predecessor (SCT, arXiv
2604.00733) collapses at 1.7B (PPL 3.6 → 75.6 at r=256). Nothing existed in
between. There is now.

## 1. Two defects had to be fixed before anything could be measured

**1a. `ffn_form="tsct"` was a silent no-op on the live stack.** The field reached
only the kda `LoopBlock`. On `block_form="transformer"` a config declaring `tsct`
built a dense model with zero `TSCTLinear` modules — and printed the dense
parameter count. Fixed and made loud (`3e5c727`).

**1b. The first A/B was confounded and was killed.** The adversarial review found
the tsct arm's init was **13.3× larger** than the block's contract: `TSCTLinear`'s
own unit-scale scheme (written for the kda loop) replaced `N(0, 0.02)`.

| | dense | tsct before | tsct after `1043573` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FFN write std at init | 0.0458 | 0.6101 (**13.3×**) | 0.0458 (**1.00×**) |
| last-block output std | 1.0253 | 2.7064 (2.64×) | 1.0257 (**1.00×**) |

A 13× larger residual stream at step 0 is a step-size test, not a representation
test. Two hours of chain were discarded rather than reported.

## 2. The rank ladder — 275M, 40 MB, b8, 4880 steps, seed 1

| arm | params | rank | BPB | vs dense |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `arch_dense275` (control) | 273.4M | — | **1.6406** | — |
| `arch_tsct275` | 110.1M | 64 | **2.1067** | **+0.466** |
| `arch_tsct275_r128` | 123.9M | 128 | **1.9376** | +0.297 |
| `arch_tsct275_r256` | 151.5M | 256 | *running* | |
| `arch_tsct275_r512` | 206.8M | 512 | *running* | |

**Doubling the rank (64 → 128) recovered 0.169 of the 0.466 BPB gap** for only
+13.8M parameters. Rank is the binding knob, not the parameter count — which is
the opposite of what a "compression" reading would predict and exactly what a
*function-capacity* reading predicts.

The arithmetic that follows: closing the remaining ~0.30 BPB by the same
doubling cadence needs r ≈ 512–1024. At r=512 the FFN costs 3·(1152·512 +
512·2848) = 6.14M params/layer against the dense 9.84M — **1.6× cheaper, not
12.8×**. At r≈820 (the rank at which TSCT parameters equal the dense FFN) the
saving is exactly zero.

**That is the night's central result: at 275M the low-rank FFN buys its 12.8×
only by paying ~0.47 BPB, and the quality needs a rank that erodes the saving to
~1.6× or nothing.** TSCT survives as a *1.5–2× memory/FLOP* mechanism at high
rank, not as the 8–13× headline.

## 3. The reinvestment arm — the budget was spent, and it did not recover

`arch_tsct275_wide`: 272.8M params and 544M FLOPs/token, matching the control's
273.4M / 545M exactly, with d=1536, L=26 and the rank-64 FFN.

| arm | params | FLOPs/token | batch | BPB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dense275 | 273.4M | 545M | 8 | **1.6406** |
| tsct275 (same shape) | 110.1M | 219M | 8 | 2.1067 |
| **tsct275_wide (reinvested)** | 272.8M | 544M | 4 | **2.1516** |

Reinvesting the freed budget into **depth** made it *worse* than the shrunken
arm, despite 2.5× the parameters — and the wide arm even had the advantage of 2×
more optimiser steps for the same bytes. Caveats, stated plainly: the wide arm
ran at b4 (activations scale with L·d, so it did not fit at b8) and without
`torch.compile`; a b4 dense control was prepared but is not in this report.

Reading: at 40 MB of data, depth is the wrong currency — the same lesson our loop
A/Bs already produced (deep+shared loses to wide+dense at equal params). If the
budget is to be reinvested at all, §2 says the place is **rank**, not depth.

## 4. Three other arms closed tonight

| arm | result | status |
|---|---|---|
| mHC in the transformer block | **+0.0853 BPB** at b16 (1.5714 vs 1.4861), +936 MiB | refused at 95M |
| AttnRes full N=L | OOM at b24, b20, b16 | two-stage memory fix landed; still **no verdict** |
| 4-bit (NVFP4) activations | max batch 24→36, peak −3.8 GiB, **67.4 vs 72.2 kB/s** | kernel kept, training arm dropped |

The AttnRes fix is worth recording because the first attempt was **forward-only**:
the review measured the *training* saved bytes as byte-identical (6.10 MiB at
S=13), so the fix could not have removed the OOM. The real fix (`0bfe05e`) makes
the mixer's own retained bytes **3,045.8 → 2.6 MiB per block** with forward and
every gradient bit-identical. Whether b24 now fits is still unmeasured.

The 4-bit result is the general lesson: **bytes/s is tokens/s and the step is
linear in batch**, so freeing VRAM cannot buy throughput. The fp4 GEMM really is
3.16× faster than bf16 at the live shape (0.32 vs 1.01 ms), but the quantize +
blocked-scale + output-scale chain around it costs more than the GEMM saves.

from low-rank FFN factorisation.

## 7. Gaps in tonight's evidence

- One seed per arm on the ladder. The deltas (+0.466, +0.169) are 15–40× the
  known seed spread, so the *direction* is safe; the exact values are not.
- 40 MB is 0.15 bytes/param at 275M — severely undertrained. It is enough to see
  a representation collapse, not enough to predict the 2 GB regime.
- The wide arm's batch/compile difference is uncontrolled (no b4 dense control).
