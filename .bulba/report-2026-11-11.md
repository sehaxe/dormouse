## 2. Blocking defect found and fixed before any measurement

`ffn_form="tsct"` **never reached `TransformerBlock`** — it only reached the kda
`LoopBlock`. A config declaring `tsct` at 275M loaded fine, printed 273.4M

## 3. The experiment, and the A/B that had to be killed first

### 3a. The confound the review caught

The first chain (01:22) measured the wrong thing. Adversarial review found the
tsct arm **was not init-matched**: `TSCTLinear`'s own scheme (unit-scale, meant
for the kda loop) replaced the transformer block's `N(0, 0.02)` contract.

| | dense | tsct before | tsct after `1043573` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FFN write std at init | 0.0458 | 0.6101 (**13.3×**) | 0.0458 (**1.00×**) |
| last-block output std | 1.0253 | 2.7064 (2.64×) | 1.0257 (**1.00×**) |

A 13× larger residual stream at step 0 is not a representation test, it is a
step-size test. Chain killed, init fixed, gate added that fails on the old code.

### 3b. The mechanism arm (identical shape, 40 MB, b8, 4880 steps)

| arm | params | BPB | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `arch_dense275` (seed 1) | 273.4M | **1.6406** | — |
| `arch_tsct275` r=64 (seed 1) | 110.1M | **2.1067** | **+0.466** |

Read this correctly: the arm holds **2.5× fewer parameters**, so most of that
delta is a parameter-count difference, not a verdict on rank-64. What it does
establish is that factorising the FFN at a fixed width does **not** buy quality
for free — the freed parameters must be spent, or the model simply shrinks.

## 4. Three arms closed tonight — two losses, one unmeasurable

| arm | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| mHC in the transformer block | **+0.0853 BPB** at b16 (1.5714 vs 1.4861), +936 MiB | refused at 95M |
| AttnRes full N=L | OOM at b24, b20, b16 — no verdict existed | memory fixed tonight (936 MiB → 78 MiB per allocation), re-run pending |
| 4-bit (NVFP4) activations | max batch 24→36, peak −3.8 GiB, but **67.4 vs 72.2 kB/s** | kernel kept, training arm dropped |

The 4-bit lesson is the general one: **bytes/s is tokens/s and the step is linear
in batch**, so freeing VRAM cannot buy throughput. Only a cheaper *per-token*
GEMM can — and the fp4 GEMM really is 3.16× faster (0.32 vs 1.01 ms), but the
quantize + blocked-scale + output-scale chain around it costs more than the GEMM
saves (0.95 vs 0.99 ms end to end).
